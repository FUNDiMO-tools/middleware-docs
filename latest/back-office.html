Middleware: your back office

Last changed in v1.0.0.

This page is for the developer of a fleet’s or a POS vendor’s own admin server, and for the coding assistant beside them: how your back office lists and manages what the middleware trusts for your vendor. Your vendor’s own roles and state; your servers and your apps; for a fleet, its stores, the terminals at them and its sign-in at its payment system; for a POS vendor, its registers and the aliases a fleet proposes for its stores; and how each principal you list gets its certificate. Each route’s fields and answers are in /docs/api/openapi-admin.yaml, the back office’s file. An agent reads the same bytes through the sandbox’s MCP server as mw://docs/back-office.md, and over HTTP as /docs/api/back-office.md.

It walks the first steps in the sandbox, where you can try them alone, then a fleet’s back office and a POS vendor’s, each in order, from the first server FUNDiMO lists for you on staging. It leaves out FUNDiMO’s own acts: listing a vendor and reading the vendor list, moving a vendor’s state, writing the allowed list, sandbox keys, voiding a store’s go-live, moving a store to another owner, and the list of sales. Where you need to know one of them exists, this page says what FUNDiMO does, and that you ask FUNDiMO for it.

Every path is under /v1. Every body is one JSON object in UTF-8, at most 65,536 bytes, and field names are the middleware’s own, in PascalCase. A field the route does not name is refused 400 unknown_field. The middleware is not the record of a payment: FUNDiMO or the terminal’s acquirer is.

Before you start: FUNDiMO lists your first server

Added in v1.0.0.

On staging your back office starts with one human act, and it is FUNDiMO’s: FUNDiMO lists your vendor’s first server, an admin one, and gives you its pairing code once: 16 characters of Crockford base32 in four groups of 4, valid 15 minutes and for one use. Ask FUNDiMO for it. The code is a secret: whoever receives it gives it to whoever runs the server, by a path no one else reads, and it stays out of every log, the server’s own among them. In the sandbox you ask for that code yourself (Trying it in the sandbox, below).

Your server then makes its own key, a CSR for it, and spends the code at POST /v1/enrol for its certificate (Certificates, below). /docs/api/onboarding-server.md walks each step with code to paste: step 1, the listing and the code; step 2, the key, in your own custody; step 3, the CSR; and step 4, enrolment. Its steps 8 and 9 keep the certificate alive, and step 10 says what a holder does after each refusal.

The certificate of an admin server reaches the routes of this page inside its own vendor: the vendor’s own roles and state, your servers and your apps, for a fleet vendor its stores, its terminals and its sign-in, and for a POS vendor its registers and the aliases that name it. It reaches no other vendor’s, and none of FUNDiMO’s own acts. Who may do what, below, gives each route’s reach. Staging takes a certificate it issued, alone, and refuses a sandbox key 401 sandbox_key_on_live.

The examples of this page run against staging, but those of Trying it in the sandbox, below. They read staging’s base URL from $MW, as /docs/api/environments.md names it, and present your admin server’s chain and key, admin-chain.pem and admin.key, which /docs/api/onboarding-server.md steps 2 to 4 make and issue. Each id ending in ... stands for one the middleware answered with. To see that your certificate works:

curl -s "$MW/v1/certificates/self" --cert admin-chain.pem --key admin.key

The answer is 200:

{ "NotAfter": "...", "RenewFrom": "...", "RenewNow": false }

Each path that names {VendorIdentification} takes your own vendor’s id, ven_...: the vendor FUNDiMO listed your first server under. Ask FUNDiMO for it beside the code. Your first store’s answer names it as OwnerVendor, and your first register’s as VendorIdentification.

To read your own vendor’s roles and state:

curl -s "$MW/v1/vendors/ven_..." --cert admin-chain.pem --key admin.key

The answer is 200:

{ "VendorIdentification": "ven_...", "Roles": ["fleet"], "State": "active" }

Roles holds fleet, pos or both. State is active or suspended, which FUNDiMO alone moves. suspended refuses your vendor’s writes and its sales, and the sales of every store it owns, while its reads answer as before, this one among them.

FUNDiMO may also create a store for your fleet, or list a register for your POS vendor. Each shows in your own listings beside your own, naming your vendor.

Trying it in the sandbox

Added in v1.0.0.

You can try your back office in the sandbox before you ask FUNDiMO for anything. The sandbox passes the routes of /docs/api/openapi-admin.yaml to the certificate of your world’s own admin server, and the middleware answers them as on staging, inside your world. Every other caller is answered 404 not_found at them: a sandbox key, alone or beside that certificate, any other certificate, and no credential. Only four of the file’s routes answer other callers too: enrolment, the daily check, renewal and the health check.

The examples of this section read the sandbox’s base URL from $SANDBOX, as /docs/api/environments.md names it. The files keep the names they have on staging, admin.key and admin-chain.pem, so keep the sandbox’s in a directory of their own: staging takes no certificate the sandbox issued.

1. Take a key, which opens a world

Added in v1.0.0.

POST /sandbox/v1/keys takes no credential:

curl -s -X POST "$SANDBOX/sandbox/v1/keys"

The answer is 201, with your key and your world’s ids (/docs/api/sandbox.md, 1. A key and a world). The key is shown this once. Put it in the file ~/.mw-sandbox-auth, as /docs/api/sandbox.md, 3. The MCP server, says: the next step reads it from there.

A world lasts 30 days, and nothing in it is a record. It is one vendor, a fleet and a POS vendor at once, and it comes with one store, live, nine terminals at it, one register bound to all nine, and the sign-in stand-in the store holds. Your back office reads all of these and makes its own beside them.

2. Ask for your admin server’s code

Added in v1.0.0.

POST /sandbox/v1/codes, under your key, gives the code of an admin server of your world’s vendor, as FUNDiMO does on staging:

curl -s -X POST "$SANDBOX/sandbox/v1/codes" \
  -H @"$HOME/.mw-sandbox-auth" \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"Role":"admin"}'

The answer is 201:

{ "Role": "admin", "PairingCode": "...", "VendorIdentification": "ven_...", "StoreIdentification": "mst_...", "Next": "..." }

PairingCode is a server’s code, 16 characters, valid 15 minutes and for one use. It is a secret. VendorIdentification is your world’s vendor, for each path that names {VendorIdentification}, and StoreIdentification is the store your world came with, which you read and do not change. Asked again before the server enrols, the route gives a fresh code. It gives six codes an hour, of every role together, and then 429 sandbox_rate. To try a renewal the same day, send "Due": true beside the role (/docs/api/sandbox.md, 4. The POS journey).

3. Enrol the server

Added in v1.0.0.

The key, the CSR and enrolment are steps 2 to 4 of /docs/api/onboarding-server.md. Make admin.key and admin.csr, then spend the code at POST /v1/enrol with the code block of Enrolling, below, at $SANDBOX in place of $MW: it writes admin-chain.pem. On staging FUNDiMO gives you the code, and the rest is the same.

Present the certificate with no Authorization header beside it. Read your stores, which hold your world’s own:

curl -s "$SANDBOX/v1/merchant-stores" --cert admin-chain.pem --key admin.key

4. A fleet: a store and a terminal of your own

Added in v1.0.0.

The steps are those of A fleet’s back office, in order, below, at $SANDBOX, but for what part 6 says. POST /v1/merchant-stores makes a store of your own (step 1 there). Take it live (step 9), and list a terminal at it by its serial (step 3):

# The answer holds the store's `mst_...` and its `mid_...`.
curl -s -X POST "$SANDBOX/v1/merchant-stores" \
  --cert admin-chain.pem --key admin.key \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"IdempotencyKey":"store-0001-a"}'
curl -s -X PUT "$SANDBOX/v1/merchant-stores/mst_.../state" \
  --cert admin-chain.pem --key admin.key \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"Event":"live"}'
# The answer holds the terminal's `poi_...` and its code, five words.
curl -s -X POST "$SANDBOX/v1/merchant-stores/mst_.../terminals" \
  --cert admin-chain.pem --key admin.key \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"IdempotencyKey":"term-0001-a","SerialNumber":"SN-0001"}'

The code lasts 15 minutes, and POST /v1/terminals/{POIIdentification}/pairing-code gives another (step 11). Your own app enrols with the code and that serial (/docs/api/onboarding-device.md, In the sandbox, then steps 3 to 6), and connects on its certificate at /v1/terminals/{POIIdentification}/connect (/docs/api/terminal.md). No attestation is required in the sandbox, so an emulator or a debug build can enrol.

A sign-in credential of your own, a key pair (step 5), is made by POST /v1/vendors/{VendorIdentification}/signin-credentials with the sandbox’s adapter and no Secret:

curl -s -X POST "$SANDBOX/v1/vendors/ven_.../signin-credentials" \
  --cert admin-chain.pem --key admin.key \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"IdempotencyKey":"key-pair-0001-a","Adapter":"sandbox_signed_value","KeyIdentification":"key-example-1"}'

A Secret sent to the sandbox, even null, is 400 adapter_not_taken. Your world’s fleet holds a sign-in profile from the start, so the create needs no step 4 first. Attach the key pair to your store as step 6 does.

5. A POS vendor: a register of your own, linked to that terminal

Added in v1.0.0.

POST /v1/registers lists a register of your own (A POS vendor’s back office, in order, step 2, below):

curl -s -X POST "$SANDBOX/v1/registers" \
  --cert admin-chain.pem --key admin.key \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"IdempotencyKey":"till-0001-a"}'

A pos server calls for it, and names it in Mw-Register on each call. Your world’s own pos server may, enrolled on the code Enrolment holds in the key’s 201, or on one from POST /sandbox/v1/codes with {"Role":"server"}. A world of a register on a device has no pos server, and takes no code for one: there, list a pos server first (step 1 there), with POST /v1/vendors/{VendorIdentification}/servers.

To sell to the terminal of part 4, the register links to it, by the word the terminal shows: POST /v1/links naming your store’s mid_..., then the word (/docs/api/linking.md). A sale then names that mid_... and the terminal’s poi_... (/docs/api/pos.md). Your world’s own register cannot link there: it sits at your world’s own store until its nine terminals are unlinked, and its ask at another store is 409 register_in_other_store.

Reading a list a page at a time

Added in v1.0.0.

Five of your lists are read a page at a time: your stores, a store’s terminals, your servers, the aliases that name your vendor, and your key pairs. page_size is the most rows a page holds, from 1 to 100, and 50 where you send none:

curl -s "$MW/v1/merchant-stores?page_size=2" --cert admin-chain.pem --key admin.key

The answer is 200, a page:

{ "MerchantStores": [ { "StoreIdentification": "mst_...", "MerchantIdentification": "mid_...", "OwnerVendor": "ven_...", "State": "live", "CreatedBy": { "VendorIdentification": "ven_...", "Scope": "admin", "ServerIdentification": "srv_..." } }, { "StoreIdentification": "mst_...", "MerchantIdentification": "mid_...", "OwnerVendor": "ven_...", "State": "onboarding", "CreatedBy": { "VendorIdentification": "ven_...", "Scope": "onboarding", "ServerIdentification": "srv_..." } } ], "NextPage": "..." }

NextPage is on every page. While it is not null, send it back unchanged as page_after, for the page after this one:

curl -s "$MW/v1/merchant-stores?page_size=2&page_after=..." --cert admin-chain.pem --key admin.key

Read until NextPage is null. Until then you hold part of the list, so never take a row missing from a page for one that is gone. NextPage is opaque: never build one or change one. A row that stands for the whole of your walk is on exactly one page, and one made during it may be missing: walk again to see it. A removed server, terminal or key pair keeps its place in its list. A parameter out of its range is 400 invalid_query. Your registers, GET /v1/registers, and every other list are answered whole.

Retrying a create

Added in v1.0.0.

Six of your creates take a key of your own, IdempotencyKey, in the body, and your admin server must send it on each: a body without it is 400 missing_field, Error.Fields naming IdempotencyKey beside any other field missing, sorted. If a create’s answer is lost, to a timeout, a 503 or a crash before your back office recorded it, send the same request again with the same key: the middleware answers with the object the first send made, and never makes a second.

A replay of each answers:

  • POST /v1/merchant-stores: your store, as GET /v1/merchant-stores lists it.
  • POST /v1/merchant-stores/{StoreIdentification}/terminals: your terminal, as GET /v1/merchant-stores/{StoreIdentification}/terminals lists it.
  • POST /v1/vendors/{VendorIdentification}/signin-credentials: your key pair, as GET /v1/vendors/{VendorIdentification}/signin-credentials lists it.
  • POST /v1/registers: your register, as GET /v1/registers lists it.
  • POST /v1/vendors/{VendorIdentification}/servers: your server, as GET /v1/vendors/{VendorIdentification}/servers lists it.
  • POST /v1/vendors/{VendorIdentification}/app-identities: your app identity as its 201 showed it, as it now stands.

Choose the key yourself: 8 to 64 ASCII letters, digits, ., _, :, / and -, such as a UUID or your own id for the object. Draw one for each object you mean to make, and keep it with the request until the request is answered. A key off that pattern, or null, is 400 invalid_field. The key is your vendor’s and its route’s: both of your admin servers hold one set of keys, so either may send again a create the other sent, and the same key on two routes is two keys. The middleware holds a key 24 hours from the create that spent it. After that it holds none, and a create under it makes a new object.

A replay is 200, the object as it now stands: it makes nothing, and it is answered before your vendor’s state is read, so a replay while your vendor is suspended is 200 too. It never holds a PairingCode: a code is shown once. If the lost answer held one, get a fresh code with POST /v1/terminals/{POIIdentification}/pairing-code (A fleet’s back office, in order, step 11) or POST /v1/registers/{RegisterIdentification}/pairing-code (A POS vendor’s back office, in order, step 4). A server has no such route: remove the server the replay names, and list another under a new key. An object you may no longer see, such as a store FUNDiMO’s operator moved to another owner, is 404 not_found.

Another request under a key the middleware holds is 409 idempotency_conflict, and nothing is written: send the first request again, or use a new key for a new object. The middleware compares the route, the id its path names where it names one, and every field of the body but IdempotencyKey, and an optional field left out is the same as one sent null or sent as the value its absence stands for. It keeps nothing of a key pair’s Secret, so it compares neither the Secret nor its SecretEncoding: sent again with another Secret, your key pair’s create is a replay, and the first Secret stands. To change a secret, replace the key pair (A fleet’s back office, in order, step 5).

A create refused, 400, 403, 404, 409, or a 503 that wrote nothing, leaves its key unspent: send it again with the same key once you have fixed the cause. A 503 store_unavailable is no verdict, so after one send the same request with the same key: it answers the object where the first send landed, and makes it where it did not. No other route of yours takes a key. An alias’s proposal holding one is 400 unknown_field, and a proposal sent again makes a second open proposal of the same id, which the POS vendor sees beside the first.

A fleet’s back office, in order

Added in v1.0.0.

A fleet vendor’s admin server, from its first certificate to its stores’ terminals and their sign-in at the fleet’s payment system. Each step names the one before it that it needs. Each create here, a store, a terminal and a key pair, carries your own IdempotencyKey, so a create sent again after a lost answer finds the object the first send made, and never makes a second (Retrying a create, above).

1. Create a store

Added in v1.0.0.

The owner is your vendor, and a store holds no name. The body holds your IdempotencyKey for the store, and may give the store’s own number at your payment system, AcceptorIdentification, which a terminal at the store carries to that system when it signs in:

curl -s -X POST "$MW/v1/merchant-stores" \
  --cert admin-chain.pem --key admin.key \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"IdempotencyKey":"store-0001-a","AcceptorIdentification":"merchant-example-1"}'

The answer is 201:

{ "StoreIdentification": "mst_...", "MerchantIdentification": "mid_...", "OwnerVendor": "ven_...", "State": "onboarding", "AcceptorIdentification": "merchant-example-1", "CreatedBy": { "VendorIdentification": "ven_...", "Scope": "admin", "ServerIdentification": "srv_..." } }

MerchantIdentification is the store’s own id. Give it to the merchant: a POS names the store by it in each sale, or by an alias its vendor accepted (step 8). OwnerVendor is your own VendorIdentification. A store takes no sale until it goes live (step 9). GET /v1/merchant-stores reads your stores, a page at a time (Reading a list a page at a time, above), each with its MerchantIdentification, and its number where it holds one:

curl -s "$MW/v1/merchant-stores" --cert admin-chain.pem --key admin.key

2. The store’s number at your payment system

Added in v1.0.0.

AcceptorIdentification is your number for the store, not the middleware’s: 1 to 140 printable ASCII characters with no space, else 400 invalid_field naming it. Absent or null at the creation, the store holds none. PUT /v1/merchant-stores/{StoreIdentification}/acceptor sets it later, changes it, or with null clears it:

4. The fleet’s sign-in profile

Added in v1.0.0.

A terminal at your store may need to sign in to your payment system before it takes a payment. The middleware holds what that sign-in needs, each value given by you, at the level that holds it: the fleet’s sign-in profile, held once for the fleet; a merchant’s key pair, attached to the merchant’s stores (steps 5 and 6); and the store’s own number (step 2) and its attachment’s UserName. When a terminal at a store that holds a key pair asks to be signed in, the middleware signs a fresh challenge with that key pair, under the fleet’s profile, and answers the terminal.

The profile holds the fleet’s application key, ApplicationIdentification, where your payment system takes one, and Profile, how a signed value is made, as that system checks it. Only a vendor holding fleet holds one. Set it before your first key pair: a key pair’s create, or a set of a store’s own (step 6), at a fleet holding no profile is 409 signin_profile_not_set, and nothing is sealed or written. PUT /v1/vendors/{VendorIdentification}/signin-profile sets it whole, on your own certificate:

curl -s -X PUT "$MW/v1/vendors/ven_.../signin-profile" \
  --cert admin-chain.pem --key admin.key \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"ApplicationIdentification":"app-example-1","Profile":{"Algorithm":"HMAC-SHA256","ChallengeLength":32,"ChallengeEncoding":"hex","SignatureEncoding":"hex"}}'

The answer is 200, the profile as the fleet now holds it:

{ "VendorIdentification": "ven_...", "ApplicationIdentification": "app-example-1", "Profile": { "Algorithm": "HMAC-SHA256", "ChallengeLength": 32, "ChallengeEncoding": "hex", "SignatureEncoding": "hex" }, "SetAt": "...", "ChangedAt": null }
The middleware’s nameWhat it isWhat your payment system may call it
ApplicationIdentificationThe fleet’s application key at its payment systemAn App key, an organisation token
Profile.AlgorithmThe MAC a signed value is, keyed with a key pair’s secret: HMAC-SHA256, HMAC-SHA384 or HMAC-SHA512Its signing method
Profile.ChallengeLengthHow many random bytes the value signed holds, 16 to 64The length of its random value, or nonce, in bytes
Profile.ChallengeEncodingHow that random value is written: hex in lowercase, base64url with no padding, or uuid, the 16 bytes as a version 4 UUID’s lowercase textHex, base64url, or a UUID
Profile.SignatureEncodingHow the signature is written: hex in lowercase, base64 standard and padded, or base64url with no paddingHex, base64 or base64url

Both keys of the body are required: a body without ApplicationIdentification is 400 missing_field, never read as clearing it. ApplicationIdentification is 1 to 140 printable ASCII characters with no space, else 400 invalid_field naming it, or null where your payment system takes none. Profile holds its four members, each required: a member absent is 400 missing_field, and any other key 400 unknown_field. With uuid, ChallengeLength is 16, and any other is 400 invalid_field naming it. SetAt is when the profile was first set, and ChangedAt when it was last set again, null until it is.

GET on the same path reads it. A fleet that holds none answers 200 all the same, every value but its VendorIdentification null. No route removes a profile: set it again to change it. A set reaches every store of the fleet at its next sign-in, and changes no key pair and no store’s attachment. A vendor holding no fleet holds none, and a set for it is 403 not_permitted, as is a suspended fleet’s set.

5. A merchant’s key pair

Added in v1.0.0.

A sign-in credential is a merchant’s key pair at your payment system: KeyIdentification, the access key id, the public half; its Secret, the other half; and Adapter, how the credential signs. It holds no profile and no application key: those are the fleet’s (step 4). One key pair may be attached to many stores of the fleet, and a store holds one at most. The middleware seals the Secret with its key service before anything is written, and keeps it only sealed. No answer holds it, how it was written or its length, and no line of the middleware’s log holds it: keep it out of every log of your own.

Set a key pair here where several stores share one, then attach it to each (step 6). A store with a key pair of its own takes one act in place of both, PUT /v1/merchant-stores/{StoreIdentification}/signin-key (step 6).

Staging is a live middleware, so its adapter is signed_value, with the Secret and SecretEncoding, how that Secret is written: text, its characters’ UTF-8 bytes, hex, pairs of hex digits in either case, or base64, standard and padded, to decode to 1 to 1,024 bytes. Keep the body in a file only your server’s own user reads, here key-pair.json, where ... stands for the secret, which this page never writes:

{ "IdempotencyKey": "key-pair-0001-a", "Adapter": "signed_value", "KeyIdentification": "key-example-1", "Secret": "...", "SecretEncoding": "text" }

Then send it to POST /v1/vendors/{VendorIdentification}/signin-credentials, so the secret stays out of your shell’s history:

curl -s -X POST "$MW/v1/vendors/ven_.../signin-credentials" \
  --cert admin-chain.pem --key admin.key \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d @key-pair.json

The answer is 201, the key pair, active and attached to no store:

{ "SignInCredentialIdentification": "sic_...", "VendorIdentification": "ven_...", "Adapter": "signed_value", "KeyIdentification": "key-example-1", "State": "active", "CreatedAt": "...", "ReplacedAt": null, "RemovedAt": null, "Stores": [], "CreatedBy": { "VendorIdentification": "ven_...", "Scope": "admin", "ServerIdentification": "srv_..." } }

Secret and SecretEncoding are both required with signed_value, and either absent is 400 missing_field. A null Secret is 400 invalid_field, as one that does not decode is. A Profile or an ApplicationIdentification in the body, which the fleet’s profile holds, is 400 unknown_field. The sandbox’s own adapter, sandbox_signed_value, is refused on staging 400 adapter_not_taken. Where sign-in is off on the middleware, a create, a replace or a set of a store’s own is 409 sign_in_not_configured and writes nothing: ask FUNDiMO to turn it on. Where the key service does not seal the Secret, the answer is 503 kms_unavailable, and nothing is written: send the same request again.

GET /v1/vendors/{VendorIdentification}/signin-credentials lists your key pairs, active and removed, a page at a time, each with the stores it is attached to now. To rotate one, PUT /v1/vendors/{VendorIdentification}/signin-credentials/{SignInCredentialIdentification} takes a body the create would take but for IdempotencyKey, which a replace does not take, and replaces KeyIdentification and Secret together, in one write: the answer is 200, the credential with ReplacedAt set, and every store it is attached to holds the new key. DELETE on the same path removes it, 204: its sealed secret is erased from the row and every store’s attachment of it ends. A removed key pair is never replaced or attached again, 409 signin_credential_removed: set a new one and attach that.

Removing or replacing a key pair here is not revoking it. Sealed copies stay in the backups of the middleware’s store until those age out, and the middleware cannot end a session your payment system issued, since it never sees one. After a leak, replace the key at your own payment system first, then send the new one here.

6. Attach the key pair to the store

Added in v1.0.0.

The store’s owner attaches its own key pair, here naming the merchant user the store signs in as at your payment system, UserName, where that system asks for one:

curl -s -X PUT "$MW/v1/merchant-stores/mst_.../signin-credential" \
  --cert admin-chain.pem --key admin.key \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"SignInCredentialIdentification":"sic_...","UserName":"user-example-1"}'

The answer is 200, the store’s credential as it now stands:

{ "StoreIdentification": "mst_...", "SignInCredentialIdentification": "sic_...", "KeyIdentification": "key-example-1", "Adapter": "signed_value", "Since": "...", "AcceptorIdentification": "merchant-example-1", "UserName": "user-example-1", "LastResult": null }

UserName is optional: absent, the store keeps the name its attachment holds; null clears it; a value sets it. A key pair of another vendor answers 404 not_found, and a removed one is 409 signin_credential_removed. LastResult is the newest report a terminal at the store made of a sign-in under this key pair: the terminal, POIIdentification; what your payment system did, Response, SUCC or FAIL; why a FAIL, ResponseReason; and At. It is null until a terminal reports, and after a replace until a terminal reports under the new key.

ResponseReasonWhat your payment system did
not_coveredIt answered that the key does not cover the merchant it was sent for. A person acts on it: the store’s terminals asking again do not change it
not_acceptedIt refused the sign-in for any other reason, or for one the terminal cannot tell apart

signature_refusedIt knows the access key id, and answered that the signature does not verify. Check the key pair’s Secret and SecretEncoding, and the sign-in profile’s Profile

GET on the same path reads the store’s credential, and where none is attached answers 200 with SignInCredentialIdentification null and the store’s AcceptorIdentification. DELETE on the same path ends the store’s attachment, 204, and the key pair stays attached to the fleet’s other stores.

A store with a key pair of its own takes one act, in place of step 5’s create and the attach above. The body is step 5’s, with an optional UserName read as an attach’s is, kept in a file only your server’s own user reads, here store-key.json, where ... stands for the secret:

{ "Adapter": "signed_value", "KeyIdentification": "key-example-1", "Secret": "...", "SecretEncoding": "text", "UserName": "user-example-1" }

Then send it to PUT /v1/merchant-stores/{StoreIdentification}/signin-key:

curl -s -X PUT "$MW/v1/merchant-stores/mst_.../signin-key" \
  --cert admin-chain.pem --key admin.key \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d @store-key.json

The answer is 200, the store’s credential as an attach answers it, with LastResult null. In one write, the act makes and attaches a key pair where the store holds none, and replaces the one it holds, under the same SignInCredentialIdentification, where no other store holds it. Where another store holds that one too, the act makes and attaches a new one at this store alone, and the shared one and the other stores stay as they were.

It is refused as step 5’s create is, and 409 signin_profile_not_set even where it would replace a key pair, which step 5’s own replace never is. A 503 store_unavailable also answers one that another act on the store’s key pair or its owner overtook, of which nothing was written. After a 503 store_unavailable, read the store’s credential back, or send the same body again: where the first landed, it replaces the store’s own key pair with the same values.

A session your payment system issued before is untouched, as by a replace. A terminal refused sign_in_not_available before its store’s key pair was attached or set, or as it was, asks no more on that connection unless a LoginCheck comes, and asks again on its next one (/docs/api/terminal.md, Signing in). A check of the store’s sign-in naming the terminal sends it one, where its app declared LoginCheck beside SASQ (step 9).

Added in v1.0.0.

Two acts on a store are FUNDiMO’s operator’s alone, and you ask FUNDiMO for each. Certificates

Added in v1.0.0.

Enrolling

Added in v1.0.0.

Each principal listed with a PairingCode, a terminal, a register on a device or a server, spends it once for its first certificate. The code is the credential, so the call carries no other: no certificate and no Authorization header. It sends a CSR for a P-256 key it made and keeps, and a device also sends its SerialNumber and its key attestation chain. Your admin server enrolled so, and so does each server it lists, here with its CSR in admin.csr:

# The code is a secret: read it, so it stays out of the shell's history.
read -r -s -p 'Pairing code: ' CODE; echo
status=$(jq -n --arg code "$CODE" --rawfile csr admin.csr \
    '{PairingCode: $code, CertificateSigningRequest: $csr}' \
  | curl -s -X POST "$MW/v1/enrol" \
      -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
      --data-binary @- \
      -o enrolled.json -w '%{http_code}')
case "$status" in
  200|201) jq -r .CertificateChain enrolled.json > admin-chain.pem ;;
  *) echo "not enrolled: HTTP $status, Error.Code in enrolled.json" >&2 ;;
esac

The answer is 201, with the principal’s own id, here a server’s:

{ "CertificateChain": "-----BEGIN CERTIFICATE-----\n...", "ServerIdentification": "srv_...", "SpiffeId": "spiffe://.../live/server/srv_...", "NotAfter": "...", "RenewFrom": "..." }

CertificateChain is the certificate and the issuing certificate. Present both at the TLS handshake of every request from then on. The certificate lasts 30 days, and renews from RenewFrom, day 15, with a new key, at POST /v1/certificates/renew. A wrong, spent or expired code, a serial not the listing’s, or a vendor suspended all answer 403 code_not_valid. If an answer is lost, send the same body again inside the code’s 15 minutes: the same code and the same key answer 200 with the certificate already issued. /docs/api/onboarding-server.md, step 4, When enrolment is refused, gives each refusal and what to do.

The daily check and renewal

Added in v1.0.0.

Once a day your server sends GET /v1/certificates/self, on its own certificate, and renews when RenewNow is true:

curl -s "$MW/v1/certificates/self" --cert admin-chain.pem --key admin.key | jq -r .RenewNow

One instance renews, with a new key, at POST /v1/certificates/renew, and the new certificate is installed on every instance inside the overlap. /docs/api/onboarding-server.md, step 9, gives the renewer’s code, and step 10 what a holder of a dead certificate does. An attestation mark

Added in v1.0.0.

A middleware set to mark issues a device’s certificate though the device’s key attestation failed, and records on it which checks failed. Only FUNDiMO’s staging middleware can be set so. Production refuses such a device, as every live middleware not set to mark does, and a sandbox marks nothing. A mark grants nothing and stops nothing: a marked terminal or register connects, links, sells and renews as any other.

A terminal’s or a register’s entry holds Attestation when its newest certificate is marked: its State, failed; FailedChecks, the checks the attestation failed, each once; and Since, when the device’s current unbroken run of marked certificates began. A certificate’s entry holds the first two. The checks are chain, root, dates, revoked, hardware, boot, app_debug and model, or chain_missing alone where the device sent no chain; /docs/api/openapi-admin.yaml says what each is. A middleware set back to refuse ends no certificate: a marked device works on the certificate it holds until its NotAfter, and its next renewal is refused unless its attestation passes every check. A re-pair or a removal stops it at once.

Refusals

Added in v1.0.0.

Each refusal the middleware gives has the body { "Error": { "Code": "...", "Message": "...", "Fields": [] } }, and nothing was done. An alias’s .../accept and .../reject, each .../pairing-code, and a store’s .../signin-check sent with no body, whose body is optional, are such POSTs.
