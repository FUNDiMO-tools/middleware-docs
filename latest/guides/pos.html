The system

FUNDiMO is the relay between a point of sale (POS) and a payment terminal. Three players take part in each sale:

PlayerWhat it runsWhat it holds
The POS vendor: youThe POS software at each register. It runs on your own server, or as an app on a device. Each register sends its sales to FUNDiMO, and reads their results.The bill, its items and the store's own surcharge. Your own reference for each sale. The private key of each of your servers and devices.
The terminal fleet vendorThe payment terminals at each store, and the app on each terminal. A terminal takes the card with its own card host.The card and the payment, between the terminal and its host. It lists its terminals at each store. It gives the merchant the store's MerchantIdentification.
FUNDiMOThe relay between the two. Your register calls FUNDiMO over HTTPS. Each terminal holds a WebSocket connection open to FUNDiMO.Each sale, its status and the references that the terminal sent. Which register is linked to which terminal. A log of every message.

A register is the POS software at one counter of a store, where a cashier takes payments. It is an app on a device, or a name that your server gives. This guide calls it a register everywhere. The register's id is RegisterIdentification, and its header is Mw-Register.

How a sale moves. Your register sends a sale to FUNDiMO. FUNDiMO sends the sale to the terminal that your register is linked to. The terminal takes the payment with its own card host. The result then goes to FUNDiMO, and your register reads it. Card, bank and personal data are forbidden in every field. What a terminal sends passes an allow-list.

Your register calls FUNDiMO over HTTPS (1, 5). The terminal holds its own WebSocket connection to FUNDiMO (2, 4). The card goes between the terminal and its card host (3, dotted).

A certificate for each edge. On staging and production, each part that connects to FUNDiMO identifies itself with a client certificate. FUNDiMO issues each certificate. Each POS server, each register on a device and each terminal has its own certificate. A certificate is valid for 30 days. FUNDiMO issues a new one from day 15, for a new private key. After 30 days, a certificate cannot renew. Its holder then needs a new pairing code from a person.

What FUNDiMO does with your server's certificate, and why it is there. It is your server's credential on staging and production, in place of a sandbox key.

  • FUNDiMO's own CA issues it at POST /v1/enrol, to the server that spends the pairing code. Your private key stays in your custody. FUNDiMO never sees it.
  • The certificate names the kind of environment, live or sandbox, the role and your server's id. Staging and production are both live. Your server presents it at the TLS handshake of every call. FUNDiMO identifies the caller from the certificate only.
  • Its scope sets what it reaches, and nothing more. A pos server's certificate reaches sales, their reads and the link calls, for the register that Mw-Register names. An admin server's certificate reaches the Operator API of your own POS vendor.
  • A register that your server lists has no certificate: your pos server speaks for it. A register on a device has its own certificate, and names only itself.
  • After a renewal, FUNDiMO retires the old certificate one hour after the first use of the new one, by default. It then refuses the old one with 403 certificate_retired.

What FUNDiMO trusts. FUNDiMO loads nothing of yours into its trust store. A server has no attestation. The pairing code is the only tie between your first certificate and your POS vendor: the server that spends it first holds the identity. Thus keep each pairing code secret.

What your software trusts. FUNDiMO's server certificate is a public certificate. Your software checks it with its default trust store, as any HTTPS client does. Never disable that check.

Thus our guidance is: automate the renewal in each live instance of your POS software that holds a certificate. Use one renewer for each certificate. A register that runs its own instance, as an app on a device, renews its own certificate. If several registers share one server, one instance renews for all of them (step 2.4).

1 Integration

The sandbox is FUNDiMO's environment for integration. It is self-help: one call gives you a sandbox key and a test world, and you do not sign up. Nothing in the sandbox is a record. No card is charged, and no money moves. Your test world is valid for 30 days. This section ends when a conformance run is green.

What you need. A shell with curl and jq. OpenSSL 3 or later, for step 1.8.

The address is a setting. Keep the address in one setting from the first line of code. Make every call from that setting. Never write an address into code. In this section, the commands read the sandbox's address from $SANDBOX:

SANDBOX=https://mw.sandbox.fundimo.com.au

The ids in every call. FUNDiMO's pages use ISO 20022's names. Your code uses them too. The addendum gives the definition of each term (Addendum).

FieldWhat it names
MerchantIdentificationThe store. Every sale names it.
POIIdentificationThe terminal that the sale goes to.
RegisterIdentificationThe register's id.
SaleReferenceIdentificationYour own reference, for example an invoice number. FUNDiMO sends it to the terminal.
IdempotencyKeyYour register's idempotency key for one payment attempt. A retry with the same idempotency key never makes a second sale.
CashierIdentificationOptional. Your id for the user of your POS who made the sale or the refund, for example a staff number. Never the person's name, email address or phone number. FUNDiMO sends it to the terminal.
TransactionReferenceFUNDiMO's id for the sale: sal_ and 32 hex characters. You read the sale with it.

1.1 Take a sandbox key

Send one call. It needs no sign-up and no credential:

curl -s -X POST "$SANDBOX/sandbox/v1/keys"
Response: 201, and a test world of your own:
  • SandboxKey: your sandbox key, sbx_ and 43 more characters. FUNDiMO shows it one time only. Send it as Authorization: Bearer sbx_.... Keep it out of logs and repositories.
  • TesterIdentification, for example sbx-3f9a1c2e7b10. It is not a secret. The names of your store and terminals start with it.
  • MerchantIdentification, your one store.
  • RegisterIdentification, the id of your one register.
  • Terminals: nine test terminals (step 1.4).
  • Enrolment: a pairing code for your server. It is valid for 15 minutes (step 1.8).
  • ExpiryDateTime: 30 days later. If you lose your sandbox key, get a new one. It comes with a new world.
Refusals
StatusCodeWhat to do
429sandbox_key_rateThe sandbox issued all the sandbox keys that it can this hour. Try again in one hour.
503sandbox_fullSandbox keys become free when they expire. Try again in one hour.
503store_unavailableFUNDiMO made no sandbox key. Retry with the normal backoff: wait 1 second, and double the wait after each failure. The maximum wait is 30 seconds. Add a small random time to each wait.

1.2 Send a first test sale

Send a sale to -approve. This test terminal always approves. Put your sandbox key in place of sbx_.... Put your TesterIdentification in place of sbx-3f9a1c2e7b10. Make a new IdempotencyKey for each sale:

curl -s -X POST "$SANDBOX/v1/sales" \
  -H 'Authorization: Bearer sbx_...' \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"MerchantIdentification": "sbx-3f9a1c2e7b10-M1",
       "POIIdentification": "sbx-3f9a1c2e7b10-approve",
       "SaleReferenceIdentification": "INV-1001",
       "IdempotencyKey": "7d0c6f4e-2b1a-4c3d-8e9f-0a1b2c3d4e5f",
       "TransactionDetails": {"Currency": "AUD", "TotalAmount": "12.50",
         "SaleItem": [{"ProductDescription": "Flat white", "ProductQuantity": 2,
                       "UnitPrice": "6.25", "ProductAmount": "12.50"}]}}'

Every amount is a string with exactly two decimals, for example "12.50". The currency is AUD. The items are optional. The terminal shows them if it can. It always charges TotalAmount.

A store's own surcharge goes inside TotalAmount. An example is 15% on a public holiday. The store sets this surcharge in your POS. Your register adds it to TotalAmount before it sends the sale. FUNDiMO has no surcharge setting, and adds no amount. The terminal adds nothing. In the basket's header, DetailedAmount, put the surcharge in Surcharge, without its tax. Put its GST in ValueAddedTax. The sum of the items is then TotalAmount less Surcharge. A bill of 50.00 with 15% on a public holiday is a sale of 57.50:

"TransactionDetails": {"Currency": "AUD", "TotalAmount": "57.50", "DetailedAmount":
  {"AmountGoodsAndServices": "45.45", "ValueAddedTax": "5.23", "Surcharge": "6.82"}}
Response: 202. It holds the sale's TransactionReference, and TransactionStatus PDNG (pending). FUNDiMO has already sent the instruction to the terminal.
Refusals
StatusCodeWhat to do
400invalid_fieldError.Fields names each field that is wrong. missing_field and unknown_field do the same. Correct the sale, and send it again.
401sandbox_key_invalidCheck the sandbox key.
403not_permittedAn id is not in your world. Check the store and terminal ids.
409terminal_offlineFUNDiMO created nothing. Send the sale again with the same idempotency key.
409terminal_busyAnother sale holds the terminal. FUNDiMO created nothing, and the idempotency key is not used. The cashier can wait, or select another terminal.
503store_unavailableFUNDiMO possibly made a sale and lost its response. Retry with the same idempotency key. Never use a new one.

1.3 Read the sale until it settles

Read the sale with the TransactionReference from the 202:

curl -s -H 'Authorization: Bearer sbx_...' \
  "$SANDBOX/v1/sales/sal_01970f6e7c3a7d3e8a4f2b1c9d0e1f2a?wait_seconds=25"

The read waits up to 25 seconds for the result to change. Then it responds. Read again until the sale settles.

Response: 200 and the sale. Its TransactionStatus tells your register what to show. Use your own words:
Waiting
For the customer, on the terminal.
PDNG: read again
Approved
Complete the sale. Print the references.
ACSP
Not paid
No payment was taken. Ask for another tender.
RJCT, any reason
Unknown
Check the terminal before another tender.
UNKN: continue to read
A sale, from start to end. FUNDiMO sends the instruction when it accepts the sale (1 to 3). Your register holds a read until the result changes (4 to 6).

With RJCT, the Reason in StatusReasonInformation gives the cause:

  • AG07: the card or the host declined.
  • AB11: no payment was taken, for a different cause.
  • DS02: your register cancelled the sale (step 1.7).

Each of these means that no payment was taken.

An approved sale has the terminal's references in PaymentReference, for example the authorisation code. Print them on the receipt. Record TransactionDetails.AuthorisedAmount as the amount charged. On a refund, record it as the amount refunded. It is the amount that the host approved, and it is equal to TotalAmount. A lower amount means that the rest of the bill, or of the refund, is still owed. A higher amount is more than you asked for. In the two cases, the terminal did not obey the contract, and FUNDiMO logs it. AuthorisedAmount can be absent.

Read a status that you do not know as UNKN. Ignore a field that you do not know.

Refusals
StatusCodeWhat to do
404sale_not_foundFor an id that FUNDiMO gave you, this does not mean "no sale". Show the unknown screen.
503store_unavailableThis tells you nothing about the sale. Read again.
400invalid_waitwait_seconds must be a whole number from 0 to 30.

1.4 Try every test terminal

Send the same sale to each terminal of your world. Use a new IdempotencyKey each time. Check what your register shows.

TerminalWhat it doesYour register shows
-approveACSP after 500 ms, with AuthorisedAmount and card referencesApproved
-declineRJCT, Reason AG07: the card or the host declinedNot paid
-technicalRJCT, Reason AB11: the terminal stopped, and took no paymentNot paid
-slowThe same as -approve, after 8 secondsWaiting, then approved
-lateAfter the deadline plus 15 seconds: UNKN, then ACSPUnknown, then approved
-silentNever responds. The sale reads UNKNUnknown
-cardThe same as -approve, with four card fields that a terminal must never send. FUNDiMO removes themApproved
-manualWaits for your response through the MCP serverWhat you sent
-yoursYour own terminal, if you also make one

The sandbox's deadline is 30 seconds. The contract's default is 120 seconds. A sale with no outcome at the deadline reads UNKN.

A sale that reads UNKN. A terminal that responds to FUNDiMO's status requests gets the result from its host. It reports the result when it knows it. If its host responds, this is before the terminal's hold ends. The hold ends 300 seconds after the sale, at the default settings. After the hold, a sale that still reads UNKN is not resolved yet. The terminal's result can still come, however late. It then replaces UNKN. Thus the cashier must still check the terminal, and your register must continue to read.

FUNDiMO's people list the sales that read UNKN. They check each sale against the terminal and the acquirer's record. Your register cannot read that list. It reads its own sales, one at a time.

The unknown outcome and its recovery, as -late shows it. Show "check the terminal" when the sale reads UNKN (2). Continue to read (3). The late outcome replaces UNKN (4, 5). While the sale reads UNKN, a payment was possibly taken.

1.5 Recover from a lost response

A network can lose the response to your POST. Your register then cannot know if the sale exists. Send the same sale again. Use the same IdempotencyKey and the same register. Send the same items in the same order.

Response: 200, and the sale as it is now. One idempotency key never makes a second sale.
Refusal

409 idempotency_conflict: the idempotency key names a sale with a different body. After a lost response, a sale can exist with that idempotency key. Thus read it as unknown, and show "check the terminal".

A lost response (2). Your register sends the sale again with the same idempotency key (3). FUNDiMO responds with the sale that it already has (4).

1.6 Send a refund

You can refund all or part of a sale that reads ACSP. Send a POST /v1/sales for the refund, with these items:

  • TransactionType RFND.
  • The sale's TransactionReference, in OriginalTransaction.
  • The amount, in TotalAmount.
  • Your own reference, for example a credit note number.
  • A new IdempotencyKey.

A sale sends no TransactionType, or CRDP. The refund's terminal can be different from the sale's terminal.

curl -s -X POST "$SANDBOX/v1/sales" \
  -H 'Authorization: Bearer sbx_...' \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"TransactionType": "RFND",
       "OriginalTransaction": {"TransactionReference": "sal_01970f6e7c3a7d3e8a4f2b1c9d0e1f2a"},
       "MerchantIdentification": "sbx-3f9a1c2e7b10-M1",
       "POIIdentification": "sbx-3f9a1c2e7b10-approve",
       "SaleReferenceIdentification": "CN-1001",
       "IdempotencyKey": "0b6e2f4a-9c1d-4e7f-8a3b-5c6d7e8f9a0b",
       "TransactionDetails": {"Currency": "AUD", "TotalAmount": "6.25",
         "SaleItem": [{"ProductDescription": "Flat white", "ProductQuantity": 1,
                       "UnitPrice": "6.25", "ProductAmount": "6.25"}]}}'
Response: 202, with the refund's own TransactionReference. Read the refund as you read a sale:
  • ACSP: refunded. AuthorisedAmount is the amount refunded.
  • RJCT: not refunded, for any Reason. This includes DS02 after a cancel from your register.
  • UNKN: nobody knows yet. Tell the cashier to check the terminal before a refund by a different method. Continue to read. Never send the refund again with a new idempotency key.

FUNDiMO keeps no total of the refunds of a sale. Your register shows the cashier the amount that it already refunded, from its own records.

Refusals
StatusCodeWhat to do
409sale_not_refundableFUNDiMO cannot refund that sale here. Refund at the terminal, or by a different method.
409terminal_cannot_refundThat terminal did not declare that it does refunds. Select a terminal that did.

These refusals create nothing. The idempotency key stays unused.

A refund. It is a sale in the opposite direction, with its own reference (2). Your register reads it as a sale (5, 6).

1.7 Cancel a sale in progress, if your register has a cancel button

A cancel button is optional. A POS does not have to have one. A register with a cancel button can cancel a sale or a refund in these conditions:

  • The sale reads PDNG.
  • The sale reads UNKN, and its terminal's hold has not ended. The hold ends 300 seconds after the sale, at the default settings.

Send no body, with Content-Length: 0. Over HTTP/1.1, the network edge in front of FUNDiMO refuses a cancel without that header with 411, and the cancel does not get to FUNDiMO. Use the same credential as for the sale: the register that made the sale cancels it.

curl -s -X POST -H 'Authorization: Bearer sbx_...' -H 'Content-Length: 0' \
  "$SANDBOX/v1/sales/sal_01970f6e7c3a7d3e8a4f2b1c9d0e1f2a/abort"
Response: 202, and the sale as it is now, PDNG or UNKN. This is not the result of the cancel. FUNDiMO has asked the terminal to stop the payment. Read the sale until it settles, as in step 1.3. The final status tells you what the terminal did:
  • RJCT with Reason DS02: the terminal stopped the payment before the payment got to its host. The sale is cancelled, and no payment was taken. On a refund, no refund was made.
  • ACSP: the cancel was too late. The payment got to its host, and the host took it. Complete the sale, and record its AuthorisedAmount. A refund that the host approved stays approved.
  • RJCT with a different Reason: no payment was taken, as on any read.
  • UNKN: as on any read, the cashier checks the terminal before another tender.

A cancel does not change a timer. If the terminal does not respond by its deadline, the sale reads UNKN. A later DS02 replaces UNKN. The terminal stays claimed until it responds or its hold ends. Until then, a new sale to it gets 409 terminal_busy.

To try a cancel, send a sale to -slow. Its 8 seconds give you the time. Cancel the sale immediately. The sale reads RJCT with DS02.

Refusals
StatusCodeWhat to do
409terminal_cannot_abortThe terminal did not declare that it can cancel a sale. Tell the cashier to cancel at the terminal. Then read the sale. A retry does not help.
409sale_not_abortableThe sale has ended, or its terminal's hold has ended. Read the sale. ACSP means that the cancel was too late.
409terminal_offlineThe terminal has no connection, or its queue is full. Cancel again, or tell the cashier to cancel at the terminal.
404sale_not_foundFor an id that FUNDiMO gave you, read the sale as unknown. Show "check the terminal".
503store_unavailableThis tells you nothing about the sale. Cancel again, or read the sale.

After each refusal, FUNDiMO sends nothing to the terminal, and the sale continues as before. In the sandbox, -silent declares no cancel. A cancel of its sale gets 409 terminal_cannot_abort, while the sale waits and during the hold.

A cancel from your register. The register asks (3). FUNDiMO responds immediately with the sale as it is now (4). FUNDiMO sends the cancel to the terminal (5). The terminal stops the payment before the payment gets to the host, and sends RJCT with DS02 (7). The register's read returns it (8).

1.8 Enrol your server for its certificate

On staging and production, your server identifies itself with a client certificate, not a sandbox key. Practise this in the sandbox. The commands of the server walk read the address from $MW:

MW=$SANDBOX

The pairing code. A pairing code is a one-use secret. A server spends it to enrol for its certificate. It has no relation to linking in section 3.

The response to your sandbox key (step 1.1) held a pairing code. It is valid for 15 minutes. If it has expired, get a new one. You can get 6 each hour:

curl -s -X POST "$SANDBOX/sandbox/v1/codes" \
  -H 'Authorization: Bearer sbx_...' \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"Role":"server"}'

A server's pairing code has 16 characters, in four groups of 4. It is valid for 15 minutes and for one use. It is a secret.

Make a P-256 private key, and keep it in your own custody. Make a certificate signing request (CSR) for it:

umask 077
openssl genpkey -algorithm EC -pkeyopt ec_paramgen_curve:P-256 -out server.key
openssl req -new -key server.key -subj '/CN=server' -sha256 -out server.csr

Then spend the pairing code. The call has no credential, because the pairing code is the credential:

read -r -s -p 'Pairing code: ' CODE; echo
status=$(jq -n --arg code "$CODE" --rawfile csr server.csr \
    '{PairingCode: $code, CertificateSigningRequest: $csr}' \
  | curl -s -X POST "$MW/v1/enrol" \
      -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
      --data-binary @- \
      -o enrolled.json -w '%{http_code}')
case "$status" in
  200|201) jq -r .CertificateChain enrolled.json > chain.pem ;;
  *) echo "not enrolled: HTTP $status, Error.Code in enrolled.json" >&2 ;;
esac
Response: 201, with CertificateChain. This is your server's certificate, then the certificate that issued it. Keep it as chain.pem, next to server.key. The certificate is valid for 30 days, to NotAfter. You can renew it from day 15, RenewFrom. If you lose the response, send the same body again within the 15 minutes. FUNDiMO responds 200 with the same certificate. Never make a new private key to repeat a call that got no response.
Refusals
StatusCodeWhat to do
403code_not_validThe pairing code is wrong, expired or spent. Get a new one.
400invalid_fieldThe CSR's signature is not valid, or its key is not P-256. Make a new private key and CSR. Enrol again within the 15 minutes.
503ca_unavailableThe pairing code is not spent. Wait, and send the same body again. Do the same for store_unavailable.
429If the body is not an Error, the network edge in front of FUNDiMO set this limit. Wait one minute, and send the same body.

After five refusals, a pairing code is unusable. Correct the cause of a refusal before you try again.

Enrolment of your server. In the sandbox, the pairing code comes with your sandbox key. On staging and production, FUNDiMO lists your first server. FUNDiMO gives you its pairing code one time only (step 2.1).

1.9 Send sales on your certificate

From now on, your server presents its certificate at the TLS handshake of every call. It names the register that it acts for in the register's header, Mw-Register. The header holds the register's id, RegisterIdentification. Do not send an Authorization header with a certificate. Make sure that the certificate works:

curl -s "$MW/v1/certificates/self" --cert chain.pem --key server.key
Response: 200, with NotAfter, RenewFrom and RenewNow. Then send the sale of step 1.2 again. Replace the Authorization header with --cert chain.pem --key server.key -H
'Mw-Register: reg_...'. Read the sale in the same way.
Refusals
StatusCodeWhat to do
401certificate_invalidYou sent a certificate and an Authorization header together. Send the certificate only.
403not_permittedMw-Register is absent or sent two times, or it names a register that is not yours.

1.10 Run the conformance scenarios, and keep the report

A conformance run does the ten POS scenarios, one step at a time. It judges each step from your world's log. It needs your certificate, and it counts only the calls that have your certificate. Thus a sale that the MCP server sends does not count. A run of a POS that works correctly takes less than 3 minutes. You operate the run with the tools of the MCP server (The sandbox's MCP server):

  1. Send conformance_start with Side: "pos", ProductName and ProductVersion. It responds with the RunIdentification and the first Step. The Step gives its Instruction, its Terminal, and what it is Waiting for.
  2. Do what the Instruction says. Use your own code, on your certificate.
  3. Send conformance_status with the RunIdentification, and WaitSeconds up to 25. It judges the step and goes to the next step. Each step has 300 seconds.
  4. When Result is no longer running, send conformance_report. It gives the signed report. Save the report.
ScenarioTerminalWhat your code does
approved-approveSend one sale. Read it until it settles
declined-declineThe same, to a decline
timed_out-technicalThe same, to a terminal that stops
unknown-lateRead UNKN. Then continue to read until ACSP
card_field-cardMake sure that FUNDiMO removed the card fields
duplicate_key-approveSend a sale again with the same idempotency key. Then send it with a different body
resend-approveFUNDiMO loses the response to your first sale on purpose. Send the same body with the same idempotency key
restart-slowAn outage of 5 seconds starts at your first read. Retry with the backoff
lost_instructionNoneNot run. Nothing changes for a POS. It reads the terminal's result as any late outcome
cancelNoneNot run. A POS does not have to have a cancel button. A POS with one reads the results of a cancel as in step 1.7

A green report shows the two scenarios that are not run as not_applicable.

Response: Result green, red, undetermined or partial. Done gives the result of each scenario. The report is Report, Signature and KeyIdentification, signed with Ed25519. sandbox.md § 6 shows how to check it with OpenSSL. A green report is a test result. It is not a certification from a card scheme.
Refusals
StatusCodeWhat to do
certificate_requiredEnrol first (step 1.8).
run_not_finishedThe run has not ended. Read conformance_status.

Sometimes the log lines of a step leave your world's log before FUNDiMO judges the step. The step is then undetermined. Run again with less traffic. A step that fails names its assertion, the seq of the log line, and what to change. mw://conformance holds every assertion.

A green run ends this section. Keep the signed report. Its SandboxVersion names the release of the sandbox that ran it. Compare it with staging's release before you start section 2.

2 Enrolment

In this section, your server gets its certificate in an environment, and your registers get their ids. Do this section on staging first. Then do it on production. The steps are the same in the two environments. Only the address changes, and with it the pairing code, the private key and the certificate. A credential belongs to the environment that issued it. Thus enrol in each environment with its own pairing code. Never use a copied private key.

You cannot enrol alone. Your first server needs a pairing code that only FUNDiMO gives, one time only (step 2.1). Ask FUNDiMO for it in each environment, and ask for an admin server (Get help). 2.1 Get your first server listed

FUNDiMO lists your first server. FUNDiMO gives you its pairing code one time only, by a path that nobody else reads. You cannot get this pairing code in a different way: ask FUNDiMO for it (Get help). This pairing code has 16 characters. It is valid for 15 minutes and for one use.

Each server is a separate identity, with its own pairing code, private key and certificate. If you list your own registers, you have two servers:

  • A pos server, for sales and linking.
  • An admin server, for your back office.

Ask FUNDiMO for an admin server if your back office lists your registers and other servers. FUNDiMO gives you the Operator API with your admin server. Your admin server can then list more servers with a call of the Operator API:

curl -s -X POST "$MW/v1/vendors/ven_.../servers" \
  --cert admin-chain.pem --key admin.key \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"Scope":"pos"}'
Response: 201, with ServerIdentification and PairingCode. FUNDiMO shows them one time only.
Refusals
StatusCodeWhat to do
409too_many_keysYou can have a maximum of two servers of one scope. Remove one first.

A pairing code that expires before you spend it does not renew. Remove that server, and list a new one.

2.2 Enrol your server

Set MW to the address of the environment. Enrol as in step 1.8. Use a new private key and that environment's pairing code. Never use the sandbox's private key on staging. Never use staging's private key on production.

Sometimes FUNDiMO refuses a new pairing code with 403 code_not_valid, although you sent it as listed and within its 15 minutes. Treat this as an alarm: possibly another person spent it. onboarding-server.md, step 4, shows how to read your servers. Compare the SpkiDigest of each server with the digest of your private key.

2.3 List each register

Your admin server lists each register at /v1/registers, the register's route:

curl -s -X POST "$MW/v1/registers" \
  --cert admin-chain.pem --key admin.key \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{}'
Response: 201, with RegisterIdentification (the register's id), Kind server and State active. Your pos server names the register in Mw-Register on that register's calls. The register can send sales to no terminal until you link it to one at the counter (step 3.1).

Give each register its own RegisterIdentification. Never send the sales of two registers with one RegisterIdentification. A retry must name the same register as the first send. If it does not, it makes a second sale.

If your register is an app on a device. A register on a device holds its own certificate. Thus your admin server lists it with the serial number on the device: POST /v1/registers with its SerialNumber. The response holds the device's pairing code. FUNDiMO's onboarding team can also list the register and give this pairing code (Get help). This pairing code is five different words of FUNDiMO's word list, /docs/api/words.txt. The words are in lower case and joined by dots, for example bronze.farm.loop.prism.tempo. The pairing code is valid for 15 minutes and for one use. The installer opens your app's enrolment screen from the app's own menu or settings, with only the device. The installer types the words, or scans them from a QR code. The app then enrols as onboarding-device.md shows. Its section "The enrolment screen" gives the requirements of that screen. In the sandbox, a register on a device is tried in a world of its own. POST /sandbox/v1/keys with {"Register":"device"} opens that world. POST /sandbox/v1/codes with {"Role":"register"} gives the register's own pairing code and its serial number. The serial number is your TesterIdentification and -register, for example sbx-3f9a1c2e7b10-register. sandbox.md § 1 and § 4, step 8, walk it.

2.4 Automate the renewal of every certificate

Your POS software must renew each certificate automatically. No person does it. Once a day, your software asks FUNDiMO if it must renew. From day 15, the answer is yes, and your software renews. After its NotAfter, a certificate cannot renew. Its server then needs a new pairing code, from a new listing. Thus put the renewal in your software. Each certificate in use must have one live instance that renews it.

The daily question is GET /v1/certificates/self, on the certificate. To test it manually, send it with curl:

curl -s "$MW/v1/certificates/self" --cert chain.pem --key server.key | jq -r .RenewNow
Response: true from day 15. Your software then does these steps:
  1. It makes a new private key and CSR.
  2. It sends POST /v1/certificates/renew on the current certificate. The body holds CertificateSigningRequest only.
  3. It installs the new private key and chain on each copy of your server.
  4. It deletes the old private key only after FUNDiMO responds to a call on the new certificate.

The old certificate stays valid for one hour after the first use of the new certificate. onboarding-server.md, step 9, has the renewer's daily script. It also has the script that each copy runs to use the new certificate.

One renewer for each certificate:

  • A register that runs as an app on a device holds its own certificate. The app renews it, as onboarding-device.md, steps 9 and 10, show. The app sets its own daily check.
  • A register that your server lists holds no certificate. Your pos server speaks for it.
  • All copies of one server use the same private key and certificate. Name one copy as the renewer, in its configuration. Do not let the copies race. The other copies get the new private key and chain from the renewer, well within the hour.
  • Any copy can ask the daily question. Only the renewer acts on the answer.

A second renewer causes problems. It retires the first renewer's new certificate, and FUNDiMO reads this as a sign of a copied private key. Or FUNDiMO refuses it with 403 certificate_retired. After 5 certificates in 24 hours, FUNDiMO refuses every renewal with 429 renewal_rate.

Refusals
StatusCodeWhat to do
409renewal_not_dueWait until RenewNow is true.
429renewal_rateFUNDiMO issued 5 certificates in 24 hours. The most likely cause is a second renewer, or a copy of your private key. Find it.
403certificate_retiredOn your newest certificate: stop, and tell a person. Something else holds a newer certificate.
401certificate_expiredThe certificate cannot renew. The server needs a new pairing code, from a new listing.

Never delete a private key or a certificate because of a refusal.

3 Link

Linking connects a register to the terminal next to it. It pairs the register and the terminal: the register's sales then go to that terminal. The calls are under /v1/links, and linking.md describes them. Link on staging first, with real terminals and test cards. Then link on production, at your merchants' stores. The steps are the same, at production's address. Only the cashier at the counter is necessary.

Linking is your responsibility. You are the POS vendor, and you link each register to its terminals. Nobody else does it for you. FUNDiMO refuses a sale that names a terminal that is not linked to the register: 403 not_permitted. FUNDiMO creates nothing, and the idempotency key stays unused. This refusal also has other causes, for example a store that is not live. FUNDiMO gives one code for all of them.

Before you link, make sure of these conditions:

  • The register is listed (step 2.3), and your pos server is enrolled (step 2.2).
  • The terminal's fleet vendor listed the terminal at the store. The terminal is enrolled and connected.
  • The terminal is not linked to a register yet.
  • The store accepts your POS vendor. Every store does this until its fleet vendor changes it.
  • Your register has the store's MerchantIdentification. The fleet vendor gives it to the merchant.

3.1 Link each register to its terminal, at the counter

Your register sends the request:

curl -s -X POST "$MW/v1/links" \
  --cert chain.pem --key server.key \
  -H 'Mw-Register: reg_...' \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"MerchantIdentification":"mid_..."}'
Response: 201, with LinkIdentification and ExpiresInSeconds, 120 by default. Each terminal at the store that is not linked now shows two words, for example rabbit.lamp. Your register never sees the words.

Ask the cashier to type the two words from the terminal in their hand. A dot or a space goes between the words. While you wait, GET /v1/links/{LinkIdentification} gives Asked and Shown. While Shown is 0, no terminal shows its words yet. Then send what the cashier typed:

read -r -p 'Words on the terminal: ' WORD
jq -n --arg word "$WORD" '{Word: $word}' \
  | curl -s -X POST "$MW/v1/links/lnk_.../word" \
      --cert chain.pem --key server.key \
      -H 'Mw-Register: reg_...' \
      -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
      --data-binary @-
Response: 200, with the terminal's POIIdentification. Keep it with the register, because the register's sales name it. The terminal shows that it is linked.

Thus a request gets 409 no_terminal_to_link. To try linking, unlink -yours, and connect a terminal of your own.

Linking at the counter. The words go to the terminal (3). Only a person who holds the terminal can read them (4). Your register sends what the cashier typed (6).

3.2 Send a first sale through the linked terminal

Send a sale as in step 1.9, on your certificate. Name the POIIdentification from step 3.1. Read the sale as in step 1.3. On staging, the cashier uses a test card. Never use a real card on staging. On production, your register takes real payments for a real merchant.

What green conformance means for your POS software

A green conformance run means one thing: your POS software did each step one time, in the sandbox. It is not a certification. On every other day, your POS software must obey these rules.

  • The users of your POS. The people who use your POS are your responsibility. When your POS sends a sale or a refund, FUNDiMO takes it that a user of the POS was authenticated to make it. FUNDiMO judges nothing about that user.
  • The idempotency key. Your register's idempotency key for one payment attempt has 8 to 64 letters, digits and . _ : / -. Make a new one for each sale and each refund. For a retry, send the same idempotency key with the same sale, from the same register. An idempotency key lasts as long as its sale. By default, FUNDiMO forgets a sale 24 hours after it stopped being pending. After that, the idempotency key starts a new sale.
  • The unknown screen. Show "check the terminal" when a sale reads UNKN. Do not wait until the reads stop. Show it also for 404 sale_not_found on an id that FUNDiMO gave you. Show it also for 409 idempotency_conflict after a lost response. A payment was possibly taken. Continue to read. Take no other tender, and no refund by a different method, until the cashier has checked the terminal.
  • No card, bank or personal data in a field that you fill. This includes an item's description and your invoice number. Send no card number, expiry, track data, BSB, account number or name. FUNDiMO cannot find these in a string that you choose. FUNDiMO logs every message.
  • One register, one id. A sale belongs to its register. Its idempotency key is unique in the register. Only that register can read the sale.
  • A cancel is a request, not a result. The 202 of POST /v1/sales/{TransactionReference}/abort is the sale as it is now. Continue to read. RJCT with DS02 means cancelled. ACSP means that the payment was taken. If the customer cancels at the terminal, the sale reads RJCT with AB11.
  • The amount charged. Record the TransactionDetails.AuthorisedAmount of an approval as the amount charged, or refunded. You add the store's own surcharge to TotalAmount. Nobody else adds one.
  • Renewal is automatic. Your software renews each certificate from day 15, with one renewer for each certificate (step 2.4).
  • Read what you do not know safely. Read a status that you do not know as UNKN. Ignore a field that you do not know. RJCT means no payment, for any Reason.

The sandbox's MCP server

What it is. The sandbox runs an MCP (Model Context Protocol) server at POST /mcp. With it, an AI coding assistant can read FUNDiMO's pages and check a sale before you send it. The assistant can also send test sales, operate a test terminal and read your world's log.

Who it is for. Your developer's AI coding assistant, in the sandbox only. Your register's own code calls the HTTP routes of section 1. A conformance run counts only the calls that have your certificate.

Connect your assistant. You can use any MCP client on revision 2026-07-28 or 2025-11-25. It connects over Streamable HTTP at $SANDBOX/mcp, with your sandbox key on every request. There is no session and no OAuth. The pages give this command for the command line of one assistant:

claude mcp add --transport http mw-sandbox "$SANDBOX/mcp" --header "Authorization: Bearer sbx_..."

Then start a new session, because a client reads its servers when a session starts. You can also call the server over plain HTTP, with no client. Keep the line with your sandbox key in a file that only you can read. Send one request at a time:

touch ~/.mw-sandbox-auth && chmod 600 ~/.mw-sandbox-auth
(read -rs key && printf 'Authorization: Bearer %s\n' "$key" > ~/.mw-sandbox-auth)
curl -s "$SANDBOX/mcp" -H @"$HOME/.mw-sandbox-auth" \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -H 'Accept: application/json, text/event-stream' \
  -H 'MCP-Protocol-Version: 2025-11-25' \
  -d '{"jsonrpc":"2.0","id":1,"method":"resources/read","params":{"uri":"mw://world"}}'

Without a sandbox key, you can read every resource except mw://world. Also, describe_field, validate_message and explain_refusal respond. Every other tool responds key_required. A sandbox key that is unknown or expired gets key_not_recognised. A staging or production credential gets 401 credential_not_for_sandbox. If you sent one, rotate it.

An example

Ask your assistant: "Send a test sale to my approve terminal, and show me its log." The assistant does these steps:

  1. It reads mw://world to get your store, your terminals and the timers in force.
  2. It checks the sale with validate_message, Kind Sale. The result is Accepted, or the refusal with the fields that are wrong and Next.
  3. It sends the sale with send_sale. The sale goes in Message, and WaitSeconds is 25. -approve approves after 500 ms, and the tool returns the settled sale.
  4. It reads read_log with the sale's TransactionReference. The log gives every message of the sale, in the two directions, in seq order. A sale_state line gives the sale's state in FUNDiMO's words.
The assistant reads your world (1, 2) and checks the sale (3, 4). It sends the sale and waits (5 to 8). Then it reads the sale's log (9, 10).

If a tool fails, its result has isError true, an Error, and a Next that says what to do. FUNDiMO never logs a tool's arguments. No tool changes a credential: sandbox keys and pairing codes come only from the routes under /sandbox/v1/.
