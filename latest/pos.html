Middleware: a POS’s sales, refunds and codes

Last changed in v0.5.0.

This page is for a POS vendor, and for whoever writes the screens a cashier sees: what the register shows and what the cashier does at each state of a sale or a refund, cancelling one, refunding, and every code a POS meets, by route. Each route’s fields and answers are in /docs/api/openapi-pos.yaml. Each code’s meaning, and what your code does next, are its row of /docs/api/refusals.json. An agent reads the same bytes through the sandbox’s MCP server as mw://docs/pos.md.

The register uses its own words. The words in these tables are an example.

A POS on its own server names the register each call is for in Mw-Register, on every sale, read, cancel and link: /docs/api/onboarding-server.md, 6. Name the register. An app on a till holds its register’s own certificate, and sends none, or one naming itself: on a sale, read, cancel or link, naming another register, or sending it twice, is 403 not_permitted (An app on a till, below).

A sale’s states

Changed in v0.5.0.

POST /v1/sales answers 202 with the sale, PDNG, and its TransactionReference. Read it with GET /v1/sales/{TransactionReference}?wait_seconds=25, which holds the read up to 25 seconds for a change, until it settles. Read a refund the same way, by its own TransactionReference.

TransactionStatusWhat it meansThe register showsThe cashier
PDNGThe terminal was sent the sale, and has not answeredWaiting for the customer at the terminalWaits, while the register reads again
ACSPThe terminal approved the whole TotalAmountApprovedCompletes the sale, and prints any references PaymentReference holds
RJCT with AG07The card or the host declinedNot paidAsks for another tender
RJCT with AB11No payment was taken, for another cause, such as: the terminal gave up, the sale was cancelled at the terminal, the terminal could not sign in for the store, or a person is signed in at the terminalNot paidAsks for another tender. After two in a row on one terminal, the register says a person may be signed in at it, or it cannot sign in, and the cashier checks the terminal
RJCT with DS02Your register cancelled the sale, and the terminal stopped it before it reached the hostCancelled: nothing was takenAsks for another tender, or starts the sale again
RJCT with a Reason your register does not knowNo payment was takenNot paidAsks for another tender
UNKNNo outcome came by the deadline, or the terminal contradicted itselfUnknown: check the terminalChecks the terminal before another tender, while the register keeps reading
  • RJCT means no payment was taken, whatever its Reason.
  • On ACSP, record TransactionDetails.AuthorisedAmount as the amount charged, or on a refund as the amount refunded. It should equal TotalAmount: a lower one leaves the rest of the bill, or of the refund, owed, and a higher one is more than was asked. Where it is absent, the outcome stated none, and TotalAmount is the amount.
  • A late outcome replaces UNKN, however late, unless the terminal contradicted itself, when the sale stays UNKN. Until then, a payment may have been taken. The deadline is 120 seconds from the sale at the middleware’s default settings, and 30 in the sandbox, whose mw://world states its timers.
  • The middleware keeps a sale 24 hours after it settles or goes UNKN, and 30 minutes in the sandbox. A read after that is 404 sale_not_found: on an id the middleware gave you, the register shows Unknown, never that there was no sale.
  • How long to read an UNKN sale: until a read settles it or answers 404 sale_not_found, each held read followed by the next, as after any answer. The cashier does not wait for it: the register shows Unknown at once, the cashier checks the terminal, and the register keeps reading. A sale that went UNKN at its deadline holds its terminal until its outcome comes or the hold ends, 300 seconds from the sale at the default settings and 75 in the sandbox. A register that restarts meanwhile reads the sale again from its own records. A sale that never settles is FUNDiMO’s to reconcile against the acquirer’s record, from its own list of the sales that read UNKN: the middleware is not the record of a payment, and a register reads only its own sales, one at a time.
  • Read a TransactionStatus your register does not know as UNKN, and ignore a field it does not know.
  • While a person is signed in at the terminal by its fleet’s own means, the terminal takes no POS sale: each one is RJCT with AB11, and nothing is taken, until that person signs out. No POS sale runs on a person’s session, and nothing tells the POS when the person signs out.

A lost answer. If the answer to POST /v1/sales is lost, send the same sale again, with the same IdempotencyKey, from the same register. The answer is 200 with the sale as it stands if the first send made it, or 202 if it did not, and the middleware never makes a second sale. No answer at all, or one with no Error body, is a lost answer too. A key is the register’s own: from another register, the same key makes a new sale. 409 idempotency_conflict means the key names a sale with a different body, so a sale may stand under it with no id to read: the register shows Unknown, and the cashier checks the terminal.

When the terminal cannot take a sale

Changed in v0.5.0.

POST /v1/sales refuses a sale the terminal cannot take now. Nothing is created, and the IdempotencyKey is unspent: send the same sale with it again once the cause has passed.

Cancelling a sale or a refund

Added in v0.4.0.

A cancel button is optional. A register that has one may cancel a sale or a refund while it reads PDNG, or UNKN within its terminal’s hold. Send no body, with Content-Length: 0, from the register that made the sale:

curl -s -X POST -H 'Authorization: Bearer sbx_...' -H 'Content-Length: 0' \
  "$SANDBOX/v1/sales/sal_01970f6e7c3a7d3e8a4f2b1c9d0e1f2a/abort"

Over HTTP/1.1, the edge in front of a hosted middleware refuses a POST with no body and no Content-Length with 411, whose body is the edge’s and not an Error, and the cancel never reaches the middleware.

The answer, 202, is not the cancel’s result. It holds the sale as it stands, PDNG or UNKN, and the middleware has asked the terminal to stop. Read the sale until it settles:

It settles asWhat it meansThe register shows
RJCT with DS02The terminal stopped the payment before it reached the host. Nothing was taken, and on a refund nothing was refundedCancelled: nothing was taken
ACSPThe cancel came too late: the payment was with its host, and the host took itApproved: complete the sale
RJCT with another ReasonNo payment was taken, as on any readNot paid
UNKNAs on any readUnknown: check the terminal

DS02 answers your register’s cancel alone. A cancel at the terminal itself, by the customer or the staff, is AB11. A cancel moves no timer, and the terminal stays held until it answers or its hold ends, so a new sale to it meanwhile is 409 terminal_busy.

Three refusals of a cancel are the cashier’s to see. Each sends the terminal nothing, and the sale goes on as it was:

  • 409 terminal_cannot_abort: this terminal cannot be stopped from the register. The cashier cancels at the terminal, and the register reads the sale.
  • 409 sale_not_abortable: the sale has ended, or its terminal’s hold has, or the terminal contradicted itself. The register reads it: ACSP means the cancel came too late.
  • 409 terminal_offline: the terminal is not connected, or its queue is full. Cancel again once it is, or the cashier cancels at the terminal.

The sandbox walks a cancel from your own code: /docs/api/sandbox.md, 4. The POS journey, Cancel a sale.

Refunding

Changed in v0.5.0.

Refund from the POS. A refund is a POST /v1/sales of its own: TransactionType RFND, the sale’s TransactionReference in OriginalTransaction, the amount to refund in TotalAmount, your own reference for the refund, such as a credit note number, in SaleReferenceIdentification, and a new IdempotencyKey. /docs/api/sandbox.md, 4. The POS journey, A refund, gives the command.

A sale your vendor’s register made and the terminal approved can be refunded by any of your registers at the same store, within its refund window, 90 days at the default settings: the whole amount or a part, and more than once, on any terminal there that takes refunds. A refund is never refunded.

At the terminal, the refund asks for a merchant user’s PIN before it is made: whoever holds that PIN at the store types it there. A refund the PIN does not pass refunds nothing: it reads RJCT, whatever its Reason. The time spent at the PIN counts toward the refund’s deadline, 120 seconds at the default settings and 30 in the sandbox, so a refund still waiting for its PIN then reads UNKN, until the terminal’s outcome replaces it.

Read the refund as a sale. ACSP: refunded, and AuthorisedAmount is the amount refunded. RJCT: not refunded, whatever its Reason. UNKN: no one knows yet, so the cashier checks the terminal before refunding another way, and the register keeps reading and never sends the refund again under a new IdempotencyKey.

The middleware keeps no total of a sale’s refunds, and does not compare a refund’s amount with the sale’s. The register shows the cashier what has been refunded already, from your POS’s own records.

Status and Error.CodeWhyThe register shows
409 sale_not_refundableThe middleware cannot refund that sale here: it is not a sale your vendor’s register made and the terminal approved, it is of another store, or its refund window has closedThis sale cannot be refunded from the register
409 terminal_cannot_refundThe terminal has not declared that it takes refundsChoose a terminal that takes refunds

Each creates nothing, and the IdempotencyKey is unspent.

A payment made at the terminal

Added in v0.4.0.

A sale or a refund made from the terminal’s own menu, and not sent by the POS, is reported to neither the POS nor the middleware, so it is in neither’s records. A refund made there leaves the sale refundable from the POS, which does not know of it. Where a refund is made at the terminal or another way, after 409 sale_not_refundable among other times, record it in your POS by hand, so the sale is not refunded twice.

Linking, and moving a till

Added in v0.4.0.

A register takes sales on the terminals it is linked to. /docs/api/linking.md walks linking at the counter, and what the register shows at each answer. Before a till moves to another store, unlink each of its terminals: a register still bound at one store cannot link at another, and is refused 409 register_in_other_store.

An app on a till

Added in v0.5.0.

An app on a till that holds its register’s own certificate sells, reads, cancels, refunds and links on the same routes, with the same bodies and answers, as a POS on its own server, and sends no Mw-Register. One it sends names itself, once: naming another register, sending it twice, or one of no register’s shape is 403 not_permitted on every one of those calls, a read among them, which no new code mends. It enrols as a device does, with a five-word code, a key in the device’s keystore and its attestation, and renews its own certificate: /docs/api/onboarding-device.md. In the sandbox it is tried in a world of a register on a device: /docs/api/sandbox.md, 1. A key and a world. A device may also run a payment terminal’s app: two apps, each on its own certificate, linked as any register and terminal are, the person reading the word in the terminal’s app and typing it into the POS app (/docs/api/linking.md).

The codes a POS meets, by route

Added in v0.4.0.

A refusal’s body is { "Error": { "Code": "...", "Message": "...", "Fields": [] } }; in the sandbox, its gate’s 404 and its 503 ca_unavailable add Next beside Error. Ignore a key you do not know. Each code’s meaning, and what your code does next, are its row of /docs/api/refusals.json, also mw://refusals, by its Code, where its Channel is Error.Code. That file is the sandbox’s: a Next that names an address names the sandbox’s own; for terminal_offline, code_not_valid and ca_unavailable it adds the sandbox’s own steps; and not_found’s and sandbox_key_invalid’s are the sandbox’s own. On a route that names an id, 404 not_found may also mean the id is not yours: on a link route, no link request or binding of the register that asked has it; on a route of your admin server, no register of your vendor has it. The five body codes are 400 invalid_json, unknown_field, missing_field, invalid_field and body_unreadable.

Beside its own, every route but GET /v1/health and POST /v1/enrol answers 401 certificate_required, certificate_invalid, certificate_expired, sandbox_key_invalid and sandbox_key_on_live; 403 not_permitted and certificate_retired; and 503 store_unavailable and edge_not_trusted. A path the contract does not name is 404 not_found, and a method it does not name for a path is 405 method_not_allowed. A route that is not a POS’s, such as one of the Operator API’s, answers a POS’s credential 403 not_permitted on a live middleware, and 404 not_found in the sandbox. In the sandbox, a route may also answer 429 sandbox_rate, 503 sandbox_busy or 503 sandbox_restarting, as /docs/api/sandbox.md, 7. Codes the sandbox adds, says; and the five routes your admin server calls are not served there, so each answers 404 not_found.

A request too malformed to be read as HTTP at all is answered by the HTTP server itself, with 400, 414 or 431 and no Error body. A later version may add codes to these lists.
