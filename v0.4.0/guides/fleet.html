The system

FUNDiMO is the relay between a point of sale (POS) and a payment terminal. Three players take part in each sale:

PlayerWhat it runsWhat it holds
The terminal fleet vendor: youThe payment terminals at each store, and your app on each terminal. A terminal holds a WebSocket connection to FUNDiMO while it is on. It takes the card with its own card host.The card and the payment, between your terminal and its host. The private key of each terminal, in its keystore. You list your terminals at each store. You give the merchant the store's MerchantIdentification.
The POS vendorThe POS software at each register. It runs on the vendor's own server, or as an app on a device. Each register sends its sales to FUNDiMO, and reads their results.The bill, its items and the store's own surcharge. Its own reference for each sale.
FUNDiMOThe relay between the two. Each register calls FUNDiMO over HTTPS. Each terminal holds a WebSocket connection open to FUNDiMO.Each sale, its status and the references that your terminal sent. Which register is linked to which terminal. A log of every message.

A register is the POS software at one counter of a store, where a cashier takes payments. It is an app on a device, or a name that a POS vendor's server gives. This guide calls it a register everywhere.

How a sale moves. A register sends a sale to FUNDiMO. FUNDiMO sends your terminal an instruction, on the connection that your terminal holds open. Your terminal takes the payment with its own card host. Your terminal then sends an outcome, and FUNDiMO gives it to the register. What your terminal sends passes an allow-list. FUNDiMO removes anything else, and logs the removal. Card, bank and personal data are forbidden in every field.

Your terminal holds one WebSocket connection to FUNDiMO while it is on (2, 4). The register calls FUNDiMO over HTTPS (1, 5). The card goes between your terminal and its own card host (3, dotted).

A certificate for each edge. On staging and production, each part that connects to FUNDiMO identifies itself with a client certificate. FUNDiMO issues each certificate. Each terminal, each POS server and each register on a device has its own certificate. A certificate is valid for 30 days. FUNDiMO issues a new one from day 15, for a new private key. After 30 days, a certificate cannot renew. Its terminal then needs a new pairing code, which a person brings.

What FUNDiMO does with your terminal's certificate, and why it is there. It is your terminal's credential on staging and production, in place of a sandbox key.

  • FUNDiMO's own CA issues it at POST /v1/enrol. The terminal shows three things. The pairing code proves that an authorised person is at the device now. The serial number is the one that its listing holds. The Android Key Attestation chain proves that the private key is in hardware. It also proves an unmodified device of an allowed model, and the app that you registered.
  • The private key is in the Android keystore, and never goes out of it.
  • The certificate names the kind of environment, live or sandbox, the role and your terminal's POIIdentification. Staging and production are both live. Your terminal presents it at the TLS handshake of its WebSocket upgrade. FUNDiMO identifies the terminal from the certificate only.
  • It renews with a new private key and a new attestation. Thus a device proves its state again every 15 days. A device that is rooted after it enrolled loses its identity within one life of a certificate. Staging can mark such a device instead.
  • After a renewal, the old certificate stays valid until the first use of the new one, and for one hour after it, by default. FUNDiMO then refuses the old one with 403 certificate_retired.

Your back office's admin server has a certificate too, as a POS server does. It reaches the Operator API of your own vendor: your stores, terminals, servers and app identities.

What FUNDiMO trusts. FUNDiMO loads nothing of yours into its trust store. You register your app's identity: its package and the SHA-256 digest of its signing certificate. FUNDiMO checks each device's attestation chain against Google's attestation roots. These roots are not yours to give.

What your software trusts. FUNDiMO's server certificate is a public certificate. Your app checks it with the device's default trust store, as any HTTPS client does. Never disable that check.

Thus our guidance is: automate the renewal. Your app renews its terminal's certificate automatically, on every terminal. Your back office's server renews its own certificate. Use one renewer for each certificate (step 2.5).

1 Integration

The sandbox is FUNDiMO's environment for integration. It is self-help: one call gives you a sandbox key and a test world, and you do not sign up. Nothing in the sandbox is a record. No card is charged, and no money moves. Your test world is valid for 30 days. The sandbox has no card host for your terminal. Your terminal and its own host decide each outcome. Use the host's test environment, or a test host of your own. This section ends when a conformance run is green.

What you need. Your terminal's app, and a shell with curl for the sandbox's calls. The pages show an Android app, with Kotlin for each step. The Kotlin uses OkHttp, the Android keystore and Bouncy Castle's PKIX library. FUNDiMO publishes no library of its own.

The address is a setting. Keep the address in one setting from the first line of code. Make every address from that setting, the WebSocket's address too. Never write an address into code. In this section, the commands read the sandbox's address from $SANDBOX:

SANDBOX=https://mw.sandbox.fundimo.com.au

The fields that your terminal uses. FUNDiMO's pages use ISO 20022's names. Your code uses them too. The addendum gives the definition of each term (Addendum).

1.1 Take a sandbox key

Send one call. It needs no sign-up and no credential:

curl -s -X POST "$SANDBOX/sandbox/v1/keys"
Response: 201, and a test world of your own:
  • SandboxKey: your sandbox key, sbx_ and 43 more characters. FUNDiMO shows it one time only. Keep it out of logs and repositories.
  • TesterIdentification, for example sbx-3f9a1c2e7b10. It is not a secret. The names of your terminals start with it.
  • Terminals: nine terminals. <tester>-yours is the terminal that your terminal connects as. Nothing in the sandbox holds it. You can operate <tester>-manual through the MCP server.
  • MerchantIdentification, your world's store.
  • ExpiryDateTime: 30 days later.

1.2 Connect your terminal

Open a WebSocket to your terminal's path. Use the sandbox's address, with its scheme changed to wss://. Put your own TesterIdentification in the path. Put your sandbox key on the upgrade. On every upgrade, also declare what your terminal does:

  • In Mw-Transaction-Types, RFND when your terminal does refunds (step 1.6).
  • In Mw-Message-Functions, SSMQ when it responds to a StatusRequest (step 1.8).
  • In Mw-Message-Functions, SSAB when it responds to an Abort (step 1.9).
wss://mw.sandbox.fundimo.com.au/v1/terminals/sbx-3f9a1c2e7b10-yours/connect
Authorization: Bearer sbx_...
Mw-Transaction-Types: CRDP, RFND
Mw-Message-Functions: SSAB, SSMQ

FUNDiMO keeps what an upgrade declared for that connection only. Thus send the two headers every time. Declare a code only when your terminal does what the code names. A terminal that declares nothing gets only sales. It gets no StatusRequest and no Abort.

Each message, in the two directions, is a text frame with one JSON object. The maximum size is 65,536 bytes.

Response: 101: the connection is upgraded. Hold it while your terminal is on. By default, FUNDiMO sends a WebSocket Ping every 30 seconds. Respond to each Ping with a Pong. If no frame comes from FUNDiMO for 75 seconds, close and connect again. Some libraries respond to Pings themselves and never show them, for example OkHttp. With such a library, set its own ping: pingInterval(30, TimeUnit.SECONDS).

After any close, connect again with the normal backoff (step 1.1). Reset the wait only when a connection stays open for 10 seconds. FUNDiMO closes connections itself. Thus your terminal must always connect again.

1.3 Get an instruction

Send a sale to your terminal, as a register does. Use the same sandbox key, and a new IdempotencyKey each time:

curl -s -X POST "$SANDBOX/v1/sales" \
  -H 'Authorization: Bearer sbx_...' \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"MerchantIdentification": "sbx-3f9a1c2e7b10-M1",
       "POIIdentification": "sbx-3f9a1c2e7b10-yours",
       "SaleReferenceIdentification": "INV-1001",
       "IdempotencyKey": "test-sale-0001",
       "TransactionDetails": {"Currency": "AUD", "TotalAmount": "12.50",
         "SaleItem": [{"ProductDescription": "Flat white", "ProductQuantity": 2,
                       "UnitPrice": "6.25", "ProductAmount": "12.50"}]}}'
Response: 202 to the call, with the sale's TransactionReference. Your terminal gets the instruction:
{ "MessageType": "Instruction",
  "TransactionReference": "sal_01970f6e7c3a7d3e8a4f2b1c9d0e1f2a",
  "SaleReferenceIdentification": "INV-1001",
  "TransactionDetails": { "Currency": "AUD", "TotalAmount": "12.50",
    "SaleItem": [ { "ProductDescription": "Flat white", "ProductQuantity": 2,
                    "UnitPrice": "6.25", "ProductAmount": "12.50" } ] } }

Do these steps:

  1. Store its TransactionReference first. Store it where a crash or a reboot does not lose it. Keep it for 24 hours or more (step 1.8).
  2. Charge TotalAmount. Show it as the total, whatever the items say.
  3. Show the items if your terminal can. Ignore them if it cannot.

Ignore any key, and any frame, that you do not know.

Add nothing to TotalAmount. Add no surcharge, tip or fee. This includes your terminal's own, and any that its merchant settings would apply. Thus disable those settings for a payment that FUNDiMO instructs. A store's own surcharge on the bill, for example 15% on a public holiday, is already in TotalAmount. The register added it before it sent the sale. Never add it again.

FUNDiMO sends each instruction one time. If the connection drops, FUNDiMO does not send it again. On a later connection, FUNDiMO asks about it with a StatusRequest (step 1.8). An instruction can come again with a TransactionReference that your terminal has already started. Do not charge again. Send its stored outcome.

One payment at a time. Sometimes an Instruction comes while your terminal is in a payment. Do not start it, and do not put it in a queue. Store RJCT with AB11 as the outcome of its TransactionReference, and send that outcome. No payment was taken for it. A payment that your terminal cannot resolve yet keeps the terminal busy (step 1.7).

1.4 Send the outcome

When the payment ends, make the Outcome with the instruction's TransactionReference. Store the outcome. Then send it:

{ "MessageType": "Outcome",
  "TransactionReference": "sal_01970f6e7c3a7d3e8a4f2b1c9d0e1f2a",
  "TransactionDateTime": "2026-11-06T15:12:40+11:00",
  "TransactionStatus": "ACSP",
  "StatusReasonInformation": { "ResponseCode": "00", "AdditionalInformation": "APPROVED" },
  "TransactionDetails": { "AuthorisedAmount": "12.50" },
  "PaymentReference": { "Card": { "RetrievalReferenceNumber": "631012345678",
    "SystemTraceAuditNumber": "000123", "AuthorisationCode": "A1B2C3",
    "AcquirerPOIIdentification": "12345678", "AcquirerMerchantIdentification": "987654321",
    "RefundReference": "klujhadsc9ph87asd" } } }

Give the amount on every ACSP. Send TransactionDetails.AuthorisedAmount, with no other field in that group. It is the amount that the host approved. It is the instruction's TotalAmount, with no change, because your terminal adds nothing to it. Give the same amount each time that you send that outcome again. Do not put TotalAmount in the group: FUNDiMO gives the total. With RJCT, send no AuthorisedAmount. No payment was taken, and FUNDiMO removes one that you send. The register records AuthorisedAmount as the amount charged. If it is different from TotalAmount, FUNDiMO logs that your terminal did not obey the contract.

1.5 Check the two sides

Read the sale as the register does, with your sandbox key:

curl -s -H 'Authorization: Bearer sbx_...' \
  "$SANDBOX/v1/sales/sal_01970f6e7c3a7d3e8a4f2b1c9d0e1f2a?wait_seconds=25"
Response: 200, with your TransactionStatus. TransactionDetails.AuthorisedAmount shows the amount that you gave, next to TotalAmount. PaymentReference shows your references, in the same group and key that you sent.

Then read the sale's log with the read_log tool of the MCP server. The log has every message of the sale, in the two directions. A fields_dropped line names each field that FUNDiMO removed from your outcome, by its path. Thus you can make sure that your terminal sends nothing extra. A sale to -approve, a simulated terminal, is a full sale that you can compare with yours.

If a value does not match its pattern, FUNDiMO removes that value only. The outcome still applies. The log names the value by its path, and writes the value nowhere.

A sale, from start to end. The register's read (4) responds when your outcome comes (7, 8). The register prints your references on its receipt.

1.6 Do a refund

Declare refunds on every upgrade, Mw-Transaction-Types: CRDP, RFND. Do this only when your terminal reads TransactionType. A terminal that ignores it would charge a refund as a sale. Approve a sale on -yours. Then send a refund of that sale to -yours:

curl -s -X POST "$SANDBOX/v1/sales" \
  -H 'Authorization: Bearer sbx_...' \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -d '{"TransactionType": "RFND",
       "OriginalTransaction": {"TransactionReference": "sal_01970f6e7c3a7d3e8a4f2b1c9d0e1f2a"},
       "MerchantIdentification": "sbx-3f9a1c2e7b10-M1",
       "POIIdentification": "sbx-3f9a1c2e7b10-yours",
       "SaleReferenceIdentification": "CN-1001",
       "IdempotencyKey": "0b6e2f4a-9c1d-4e7f-8a3b-5c6d7e8f9a0b",
       "TransactionDetails": {"Currency": "AUD", "TotalAmount": "6.25"}}'
Response: your terminal gets an Instruction with TransactionType RFND and its own TransactionReference. OriginalTransaction holds the sale, with the references that the sale's approval had.

Do these steps:

  1. Read TransactionType first.
  2. Store the refund's reference.
  3. Refund TotalAmount against that sale. Use the sale's references if your host can. If your host cannot, the customer presents the card.
  4. Report the refund in an Outcome, with the refund's own reference. Never use the sale's reference.

Send one of these results:

  • ACSP, when the full amount was refunded. AuthorisedAmount is the amount refunded, the refund's TotalAmount.
  • RJCT with AG07, when the host declined.
  • RJCT with DS02, when your terminal stopped the refund before it got to the host, after an Abort (step 1.9).
  • RJCT with AB11, when no refund was made for a different cause.

FUNDiMO keeps no total of the refunds of a sale. A host that has a limit declines the refund that goes over the limit. The other cases of a refund are the same as a sale's. For a refund with an unknown result, get the result as in step 1.7. For a refund that your terminal did not receive, FUNDiMO asks as in step 1.8.

1.7 Get the result of an unknown payment

Sometimes your terminal does not know if a payment was taken, for example because the host did not respond. Your terminal must then get the result. It must never stop with no result.

An unknown outcome and its recovery. The host does not respond (2). Your terminal sends nothing, and the register reads UNKN at the deadline (3). Your terminal asks the host (4, 5). Its late outcome replaces UNKN (6, 7).
  1. Send nothing for that sale yet. Your terminal is still busy. Start no other payment.
  2. Ask the host for the payment, with the reference that you stored before the card. If the host approved, send ACSP. If the host declined, send RJCT with AG07. Send RJCT with AB11 for "never received" only if the host documents that response as final.
  3. Sometimes the host cannot respond to the enquiry, or has no enquiry. Sometimes it gives a "not found" that it does not document as final. In these cases, reverse the payment. Send RJCT with AB11 only after the host confirms the reversal. A reversal in a queue can fail, and then the customer pays two times.
  4. Try the enquiry and the reversal again until one of them gives a result. Send the result immediately, however late. It replaces the UNKN that the register reads after the deadline. At the default settings, a host that responds lets your terminal report before the hold ends. The hold ends 300 seconds after the instruction. A terminal that was off sends the result on its first connection after that.

Do the same for a refund. After a reversal, send RJCT with AB11 only after the host confirms it.

The sandbox's deadline is 30 seconds. The contract's default is 120 seconds. Some sales take more time, for example a card with PIN entry. Such a sale reads UNKN at 30 seconds. Your outcome then replaces UNKN.

1.8 Send again after a drop, and respond to a StatusRequest

After each new connection, send your most recent outcome again, with no change. Do this whether or not you think that it arrived. Stop in these conditions:

  • A new Instruction arrives.
  • A Refusal other than terminal_not_permitted comes for the outcome.
  • 23 hours pass after you first sent it.

FUNDiMO sends each instruction one time. Thus FUNDiMO asks about an instruction that a drop possibly lost. When your terminal responds to a StatusRequest, declare SSMQ on every upgrade (step 1.2). FUNDiMO starts at the connection's first Ping, 30 seconds after the connection opens, by default. FUNDiMO asks about each sale and refund of your terminal with these conditions:

  • FUNDiMO still holds it open, PDNG or UNKN.
  • It was made in the last 23 hours, while an earlier connection held your terminal.

FUNDiMO asks about the oldest first. It asks about a maximum of 4 at each Ping. It asks about each one a maximum of one time on a connection.

{ "MessageType": "StatusRequest",
  "TransactionReference": "sal_01970f6e7c3a7d3e8a4f2b1c9d0e1f2a",
  "SecondsSinceInstruction": 34 }

SecondsSinceInstruction is the time in whole seconds, from the creation of the sale to this frame, by FUNDiMO's clock. Respond with a usual Outcome, or with nothing. What you send depends on what your terminal holds for the TransactionReference:

What your terminal holdsWhat it does
An outcomeIt sends that outcome, if it did not send it already on this connection
A payment that it has not finishedIt sends nothing until it knows the result. Then it sends that outcome, one time (step 1.7)
No record, and its store of references goes back more than SecondsSinceInstructionIt stores RJCT with AB11 as the outcome of that reference, and sends it. It never starts a later Instruction with that reference. It sends that outcome for it, if it did not send it already on that connection
No record, and its store does not go back that farIt sends nothing. It cannot prove that it did not receive the Instruction

Keep every reference for 24 hours. Your terminal keeps every TransactionReference that it received, with its outcome if it has one. It keeps them for 24 hours or more. It knows how far back its store goes. This is the latest of these times:

  • the time when the store started;
  • the time when it stored the newest reference that it later deleted;
  • the time when it lost any reference in a different way, for example one that it received and could not store.

A reset that clears the store starts it again. A restart also starts it again, if the full store and those times do not stay. Measure the age of those times with a clock that never jumps, for example a monotonic clock. Never use the time of day. Thus your terminal never sends RJCT with AB11 for a reference that it possibly received.

One outcome for each reference on a connection. FUNDiMO applies a response as any outcome. A late outcome replaces UNKN, and a repeat changes nothing. A different result is a contradiction, and the sale then reads UNKN permanently. Send the outcome of a reference a maximum of one time on one connection. This applies whether a StatusRequest, an Abort or nothing asked for it. Never send it on a timer.

To see a StatusRequest, send a sale to -yours. Close your terminal's connection before it responds. Connect again with SSMQ declared. At the first Ping, FUNDiMO asks about the sale. A conformance run makes a sale that your terminal never receives (step 1.11).

An instruction that a drop lost (2). Your terminal connects again, with SSMQ declared (3, 4). At the first Ping, FUNDiMO asks about the sale (5). Your terminal holds no record of it, and its store goes back more than SecondsSinceInstruction. Thus it sends RJCT with AB11 (6). The register reads that no payment was taken (7).

1.9 Stop a payment on an Abort

A register can cancel a sale or a refund in progress. When your terminal responds to an Abort, declare SSAB on every upgrade (step 1.2). Until it does, FUNDiMO refuses a register's cancel with 409 terminal_cannot_abort. FUNDiMO then sends nothing to your terminal. On a connection that declared SSAB, FUNDiMO sends one Abort for each cancel that it accepts. It does this while the sale still holds your terminal. The Abort waits behind any frame already in the queue. Thus an Abort never arrives before its own Instruction:

{ "MessageType": "Abort", "TransactionReference": "sal_01970f6e7c3a7d3e8a4f2b1c9d0e1f2a" }

To try it, send a sale to -yours. Hold its payment, with no card presented. Cancel the sale as a register does, with your sandbox key. Send no body, with Content-Length: 0. Over HTTP/1.1, the network edge in front of FUNDiMO refuses a cancel without that header with 411:

curl -s -X POST -H 'Authorization: Bearer sbx_...' -H 'Content-Length: 0' \
  "$SANDBOX/v1/sales/sal_01970f6e7c3a7d3e8a4f2b1c9d0e1f2a/abort"
Response: 202 to the call, with the sale as it is now. Your terminal gets the Abort. Respond with a usual Outcome, or with nothing. What you send depends on what your terminal holds for the TransactionReference:

What green conformance means for your terminal software

A green run means one thing: your terminal software did each step one time, in the sandbox. It is not a certification. On every other day, your terminal software must obey these rules.

  • One payment at a time. Do not start or queue an Instruction that comes during a payment. Store RJCT with AB11 as its outcome, and send it. A queue can make a customer pay two times.
  • Store first, and keep it. Store the TransactionReference before the card, and the Outcome before you send it. A crash or a reboot must not lose them. Keep every reference with its outcome for 24 hours or more. Know how far back your store goes.
  • Never charge two times. Never charge again for a TransactionReference that your terminal has already started, also after a restart. Send its stored outcome.
  • The truth, or nothing yet. ACSP means the full TotalAmount, given in AuthorisedAmount with no change. There is no partial approval, and no added tip, surcharge or fee. Send RJCT, always with its Reason, only when no payment was taken. Send DS02 only after an Abort. If your terminal does not know, it sends nothing yet. It gets the result from its host by an enquiry or a confirmed reversal, and sends it, however late.
  • Send again safely. After every new connection, send your most recent outcome again, with no change. Send a reference's outcome a maximum of one time on a connection, and never on a timer. A different result for the same sale is a contradiction: the sale then reads UNKN.
  • Respond to what you declare. Declare SSMQ and SSAB only when your terminal responds to each. Stop only a payment that is not with its host yet.
  • One connection for each terminal id. Two connections for one id replace each other.
  • Renew automatically, and never delete your private key after a refusal. Your app renews each terminal's certificate from day 15 (step 2.5). Make a new private key only to renew, or when a person brings a new pairing code.
  • An enrolment screen that each installer can open. An item of your app's own menu or settings opens it, with only the device. It stays there after the terminal is enrolled (step 1.10).

Never send a card number, an expiry, track data, a cardholder name or a bank account. FUNDiMO forbids them in every field, but its patterns cannot stop all of them. For example, a 12 digit card number matches a retrieval reference. FUNDiMO would log it, send it to the register and keep it with the sale. FUNDiMO removes any other field before it logs anything, and logs only the field's name. A printed card line stays on your terminal's own receipt.

The sandbox's MCP server

What it is. The sandbox runs an MCP (Model Context Protocol) server at POST /mcp. With it, an AI coding assistant can read FUNDiMO's pages and check an outcome before your terminal sends it. The assistant can also send test sales, operate a terminal on -manual and read your world's log.

Who it is for. Your developer's AI coding assistant, in the sandbox only. Your terminal's own code holds the WebSocket of section 1. A conformance run counts only what has your certificate.

Connect your assistant. You can use any MCP client on revision 2026-07-28 or 2025-11-25. It connects over Streamable HTTP at $SANDBOX/mcp, with your sandbox key on every request. There is no session and no OAuth. The pages give this command for the command line of one assistant:

claude mcp add --transport http mw-sandbox "$SANDBOX/mcp" --header "Authorization: Bearer sbx_..."

Then start a new session, because a client reads its servers when a session starts. You can also call the server over plain HTTP, with no client. Keep the line with your sandbox key in a file that only you can read. Send one request at a time:

touch ~/.mw-sandbox-auth && chmod 600 ~/.mw-sandbox-auth
(read -rs key && printf 'Authorization: Bearer %s\n' "$key" > ~/.mw-sandbox-auth)
curl -s "$SANDBOX/mcp" -H @"$HOME/.mw-sandbox-auth" \
  -H 'Content-Type: application/json' \
  -H 'Accept: application/json, text/event-stream' \
  -H 'MCP-Protocol-Version: 2025-11-25' \
  -d '{"jsonrpc":"2.0","id":1,"method":"resources/read","params":{"uri":"mw://world"}}'

Without a sandbox key, you can read every resource except mw://world. Also, describe_field, validate_message and explain_refusal respond. Every other tool responds key_required. A sandbox key that is unknown or expired gets key_not_recognised. A staging or production credential gets 401 credential_not_for_sandbox. If you sent one, rotate it.

An example

Ask your assistant: "Do a sale on my manual terminal, and show me its log." The assistant does these steps:

  1. It sends a sale with send_sale, to your <tester>-manual terminal. The instruction waits there.
  2. It reads the instruction with terminal_next_instruction. It gets the Instruction frame, as your terminal would get it.
  3. It responds with terminal_send_outcome and an ACSP outcome. The tool sends the outcome as written. Thus the assistant gives AuthorisedAmount in it. The tool tells if the outcome was Sent, any Refusal, and what FUNDiMO removed (Dropped).
  4. It reads the sale with read_sale, as the register does. Then it reads the log with read_log. The log gives every message of the sale, in the two directions, in seq order.
The assistant sends a sale to -manual (1, 2). It reads the instruction as your terminal would (3, 4), and responds to it (5, 6). It reads the sale as the register does (7, 8). Then it reads the sale's log (9, 10).

If a tool fails, its result has isError true, an Error, and a Next that says what to do. FUNDiMO never logs a tool's arguments. No tool changes a credential: sandbox keys and pairing codes come only from the routes under /sandbox/v1/.
